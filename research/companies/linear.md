# Linear

## 1. Snapshot
Linear builds "the product development system for teams and agents": a fast, opinionated issue tracking, project management, and AI-agent orchestration platform for software teams. Founded 2019, remote-first (no single HQ, optional co-working hubs in San Francisco, New York, and London), team distributed across North America, Europe, and Australia and "continuing to grow internationally" (Linear Product Engineer posting, job id 4668, fetched 2026-08-25). Linear's own most recent published headcount is a "99-person team" spanning "more than 15 countries and 10 time zones" as of October 29, 2025 (linear.app/now/designing-remote-work-at-linear); third-party data brokers (Tracxn, LeadIQ, getlatka, jobsbyculture) report wildly inconsistent 2026 figures ranging from about 118 to 271 employees, none of them Linear-published, so current headcount should be treated as "roughly 100 and growing" rather than a precise number. Raised an $82M Series C in June 2025 at a $1.25B valuation (led by Accel; Sequoia, 01 Advisors, Seven Seven Six, Designer Fund, Index Ventures participating), $134.2M raised to date (TechCrunch, June 10 2025). No further funding round is confirmed by Linear or by reputable press as of this research; a third-party sales-intelligence listing (salestools.io) claims a "$50M Series D at a $650M valuation" dated October 15, 2025, but that would be a large down round from the June 2025 Series C with no corroboration from Linear, TechCrunch, Sequoia, or Crunchbase/Sacra, so it is flagged here as unverified and likely inaccurate rather than reported as fact. Used by 40,000+ companies as of August 2026 (up from 30,000+ cited in the same posting a year earlier), including OpenAI, Coinbase, Ramp, Cursor, and Scale AI.

## 2. Core narrative
Three ex-Airbnb, Coinbase, and Uber product people (Karri Saarinen, Tuomas Artman, Jori Lallo) got frustrated building software inside high-growth companies with clunky, bloated tools, so in 2019 they set out to build the tool they themselves wished existed: fast, elegant, and opinionated rather than a checklist of enterprise features (Sequoia Capital profile, Contrary Research). That founder-level product taste, obsessive craft, and refusal to follow standard SaaS growth playbooks (no A/B testing, no growth hacks, deliberately slow and profitable team growth) is Linear's defining story, and by 2026 it has hardened into an aggressive, publicly stated bet: CEO Karri Saarinen has said "issue tracking is dead" and that agents, not humans doing procedural work, are the future of how software gets built inside Linear (The Register, March 26 2026, covering Linear's "issue tracking is dead" campaign at linear.app/next). Coding agents were already installed in 75% of Linear's own enterprise workspaces by March 2026, with agent work volume growing five-fold in three months (same source), and Linear has spent essentially every product release since then, Coding Sessions, Diffs, Loops, agent-assisted document editing, deepening that bet rather than hedging it.

## 3. Mission & product
Linear's own framing: "designed for the AI era," built for "workflows shared by humans and agents, from drafting PRDs to pushing PRs" (linear.app homepage; Product Engineer posting, job 4668). The product spans intake (turning feedback into issues), planning (roadmaps, initiatives, cycles, and now "Team initiatives" for cross-team strategic planning, launched August 13 2026), building (Linear Agent, which can write, review, and test code via Coding Sessions), and monitoring (dashboards, analytics). "Loops," introduced July 20 2026, lets teams describe recurring automated jobs in plain language rather than configuring rules by hand (linear.app/now, Nan Yu). Business model is B2B SaaS with tiered plans; Agent and Skills ship on all plans, while Automations, Code Intelligence, and Guided Reviews (generally available for Business+ plans as of July 30 2026) are gated to Business/Enterprise. Enterprise buyers can now scope Linear's MCP server through their existing Okta-managed identity, with permissions applied automatically (changelog, August 13 2026). Customers skew toward high-craft, high-velocity software teams and AI-native companies: OpenAI, Coinbase, Ramp, Cursor, Scale AI, and Perplexity are named repeatedly across Linear's own materials, and an August 11 2026 customer story frames Coinbase's engineering org as betting on "agent-first development," describing efforts to move work out of IDEs entirely (linear.app/blog).

## 4. Culture & voice
Linear's public voice is literary and mission-driven rather than corporate. Its "Read Me" origin story talks about restoring "the magic of software" against three "dark forces" eroding it (noise, mediocre design, outdated methodology), framed as a "call to adventure" for engineers and designers as "protagonists" (linear.app/readme). Its hiring philosophy names four traits: craft, judgment, ownership, clarity, explicitly favoring "slope over credentials," and a refreshed April 28 2026 post ("How we hire at Linear," attributed to Doug Parker) reiterates that "hiring well matters more than hiring fast" and that "the best talker in the room isn't always the best doer," which is why paid work trials remain central to the process (linear.app/now/how-we-hire-at-linear). Teams are deliberately small and assembled per project, there are no OKRs ("one product team, one roadmap"), weekly written updates replace status meetings, and quality is enforced with a zero-bugs policy and a running "Quality Wednesdays" practice (linear.app/now/designing-remote-work-at-linear). That same October 29 2025 post notes Linear's annual company-wide offsite, held in Copenhagen in 2025, with past offsites in Mexico City, Helsinki, and Lisbon, a small but concrete, sourced signal that the company has convened in Mexico before, though it is not evidence about where Linear currently hires full-time staff. Linear gave its team its first taste of liquidity via a tender offer, announced August 27 2025 (linear.app/now). CEO Karri Saarinen has publicly framed company-building itself as "a kind of craft" and says Linear deliberately resisted standard startup playbooks (linear.app/now/building-our-way).

## 6. Engineering & AI direction
From the actual "Product Engineer" job posting (job 4668, Ashby id 0c7c2e26-0a98-42cf-a47c-9a3999fb513b, published June 9 2026) and Linear's engineering content:
- Stack for this role specifically: React, MobX, and StyleX on the frontend (a change from the styled-components reference in Linear's earlier AI-role posting); Node, PostgreSQL, Temporal, and Redis on the backend; a proprietary websocket-based real-time sync engine built for speed and offline support; an extensive, highly trafficked GraphQL API and SDK; hosted on Google Cloud with Kubernetes; GitHub, Slack, and Notion for internal tooling (job 4668 posting text). An "agentic workflow system utilizing Temporal, operating at production scale" powers Linear's own AI agents (same posting).
- The team explicitly frames itself as full-stack generalists working "in TypeScript end-to-end" on a small team, with no heavy PM overhead and high individual ownership expected from day one (job 4668 posting).
- Agent architecture, from Linear's own August 10 2026 deep dive: a custom-built agent stack (not an off-the-shelf framework) combining a system prompt for tone and hard boundaries, tool design that encodes constraints into parameters rather than prompts, a "product model" giving the agent explicit data tools plus concept explanations since Linear's product surface is underrepresented in model training data, skill-based scoping to keep context focused, and a custom harness supporting dynamic tool injection and asynchronous sub-agents (linear.app/now/how-we-built-linear-agent).
- Linear publishes Agent Interaction Guidelines (AIG), launched July 30 2025 alongside an Agent Interaction SDK, a public, evolving framework of six principles for any developer building agents on Linear's platform: disclose identity (agents must never be mistaken for a person), work through standard platform UI patterns, give immediate feedback on being invoked, expose transparent internal state/reasoning, respect disengagement (stop immediately when asked), and preserve human accountability for outcomes (linear.app/developers/aig; Linear changelog, 2025-07-30).
- Linear's MCP server exposes finding, creating, and updating issues, projects, and comments to any compatible AI client (Claude, Cursor, etc.), with example workflows for roadmap planning, standup automation, and bug investigation (linear.app/docs/mcp); enterprise customers can now govern MCP access through Okta-managed identity (changelog, August 13 2026).
- Recent infra work includes migrating parts of the sync/read path to turbopuffer for lower latency (Linear blog, August 18 2026), continuing the public engineering narrative that started with cofounder Tuomas Artman's 2023 talk on the sync engine.
- AI bet, in Linear's own words from the AI-focused sibling posting: "building intelligence into the core of Linear, enabling the product to orchestrate coding, proactively move work forward, and power-up every software team," transforming foundation models into "structured, reliable workflows embedded deeply in the core of Linear" while staying "fast, intuitive, and opinionated."

## 8. Watch-outs

You can work from anywhere within this region," without defining member countries. Every "North America"-labeled Linear posting on Ashby (checked across all 32 open roles on August 25 2026) carries a structured schema.org address field of "United States" as its country, while every "Europe"-labeled posting carries "European Union." That pattern looks like an ATS default tied to the region label rather than a deliberate country restriction (Ashby's structured data collapses each broad region to one representative country across the board), but it is not proof either way, and Santiago's own profile.yaml already flags this exact ambiguity as something to confirm directly rather than assume.
