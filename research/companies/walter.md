# Walter (gowalter.co), dossier for job id 15778, "Senior Full Stack AI Engineer"

## IDENTIFICATION WARNING, read first

"Walter" is a common company name (there is also a 100+ year old German precision-tools
manufacturer called Walter, an insurance agency "Walter Agency Inc" in Pennsylvania, a UK
butcher HG Walter, a hardware-onboarding company "Walter!"/wearewalter.com, and others).
Indeed's aggregated "Walter" company page mixes the German tools manufacturer's description
with job postings that are actually this Workable account, which is a real risk of
misidentification if you do not go source by source.

I traced identity from the live posting itself: shortcode `78D12B0E7E` resolves to Workable
subdomain `walter-careers`, canonical URL `https://apply.workable.com/walter-careers/j/78D12B0E7E`.
A duplicate listing of the same role (different shortcode `DC9A929C10`, same title, mirrored on
`jobs.workable.com`) links out to the company's own site,
`https://walter-dev.webflow.io/` (a Webflow staging domain), which in turn links to the
production site `https://www.gowalter.co/`. Both sites carry identical branding, copy, and the
same "Apply to Open Roles" link back to `apply.workable.com/walter-careers/`. That closes the
loop: this Walter is **gowalter.co, a remote-staffing/recruiting agency for Latin America and
Philippines talent**, not the German tools maker or any other Walter. Confidence: high.

## 1. Snapshot

Walter (gowalter.co) is a remote staffing / recruiting agency that places Latin American and
Philippine talent (developers, marketers, sales, support, accounting, virtual assistants) with
mostly US-based client companies, charging clients a one-time placement fee rather than running
payroll itself [gowalter.co homepage, gowalter.co/get-started]. It is a sister brand of We Work
Remotely (WWR), the long-running remote job board; both are led by the same CEO, Bryndis
Henrikson [gowalter.co/about-us; LinkedIn search result "Bryndis Henrikson - CEO @ We Work
Remotely & Walter"]. HQ signal points to Canada: We Work Remotely's BBB filing lists a Victoria,
BC and Vancouver, BC address with Henrikson as CEO [BBB profile for We Work Remotely, filed
2025-05-06], and this specific job's own location metadata on Workable lists "Canada (Remote)"
as the primary posting country [apply.workable.com/walter-careers/jobs/view/78D12B0E7E.md].
No funding, revenue, or headcount figures for Walter itself were found anywhere in this research;
that absence is itself a finding (see section 8).

## 2. Mission & product

Walter's own positioning: "Expand Your Team, Shrink Your Overheads." It recruits and vets
LATAM/Philippines candidates for client companies, pitching savings of "up to 80% compared to
North American salaries," a one-time placement fee (no recurring agency retainer), and a
12-month replacement guarantee if a hire does not work out [gowalter.co homepage;
gowalter.co/about-us; gowalter.co/get-started]. It is not a product company; it does not build
or sell software. Client-facing case study: Stamped, an e-commerce SaaS company (customer
reviews and loyalty tools for Shopify merchants), used Walter to fill two Shopify app developer
roles (one senior/lead, one intermediate) in about four weeks from kickoff to signed contracts
[gowalter.co/blog, "Not Magic, Just Strategy: How We Hire Top Talent... A Case Study with
Stamped"]. Another public testimonial is from the CEO of Conference Badge, a small events-tech
business [gowalter.co homepage]. The site also lists large logos (Airbus, Uber, Deloitte,
Unilever, Facebook, IBM, NASA) under a generic "our clients" banner with no explanation of the
engagement; I could not verify these are direct Walter placements versus aspirational/marketing
logos, so treat that list with real skepticism (unverified).

## 6. Employment mechanism for a Mexico-based hire

Walter's own "Get Started" page describes a placement-fee model: Walter runs discovery,
screening, and a shortlist; the **client company makes the final hiring decision**; Walter's
compensation is a one-time placement fee to the client, not an ongoing markup on your pay
[gowalter.co/get-started]. The Stamped case study specifically describes the outcome as "signed
contracts" between the client and the hires [gowalter.co blog, Stamped case study]. Taken
together, this reads as **Walter is a recruiter/headhunter that places candidates directly with
the client**, not an EOR that employs you itself and not a staffing firm that keeps you on its
own payroll while billing the client hourly. However, Walter's own marketing pages do not state
this explicitly anywhere I could find, and no page defines the contract type (contractor vs.
direct employee vs. EOR) for a specific engagement. **Unverified: the exact employment mechanism
for this specific role**, because the actual employer is not named in the posting; you would only
learn it once Walter matches you to a client. No benefits are stated anywhere in Walter's public
materials for candidates it places.

## 7. Compensation

The req itself lists no salary (confirmed: Adzuna's copy shows `salary_min`/`salary_max` null,
currency MXN by default tagging only, not an actual quoted figure). The one hard compensation
data point available is from Walter's own Stamped case study: a "traditional US Shopify
developer salary" was quoted at roughly $110,000/year, and Walter says it delivered "70% savings
on payroll" for that placement [gowalter.co blog, Stamped case study]. Read plainly, that implies
the actual placed developer(s), including a senior/lead-level hire, were paid in the ballpark of
$110,000 x (1 minus 0.70) = **roughly $33,000/year**, or about 566,000 MXN/year at 17.1 MXN/USD.
Walter's homepage repeats "save up to 80%" as a general figure across roles, which would imply an
even lower number for some placements. This is a single case study, for a different role
(Shopify app developer, not full-stack AI engineer) and different client, so it is a directional
signal, not a guaranteed number for this specific req. Directionally, though, it strongly
suggests Walter's placement bands sit well **below Santiago's $70,000/year LATAM-scope floor**,
likely by half or more. This is the single biggest reason to be skeptical of investing much time
here without an early, explicit compensation conversation.

## 9. Reputation, risks, things to know before investing time

- **You would not be working for Walter.** Walter is the recruiter; the real employer is
  whichever (currently unnamed) client company it matches you with. That means "why this
  company" and "why you fit" cannot really be answered about Walter as an employer, only about
  Walter as a process, and the actual day-to-day company is unknown until later in the funnel.
- **Compensation risk is real and specific, not just generic LATAM-arbitrage worry.** Walter's
  own published case study points to roughly $33,000/year for a comparable senior developer
  placement, well under Santiago's $70,000 floor (see section 7). This is the clearest reason to
  set expectations low or to raise pay explicitly and early rather than investing in a tailored
  cover letter first.
- **Thin public footprint for the agency itself.** No funding data, no employee count, no
  Glassdoor/Trustpilot reviews specific to gowalter.co were found (search returned unrelated
  "Walter" companies: HG Walter the London butcher, "Walter!" the hardware-onboarding company,
  Walter Tools the German manufacturer). A ScamAdviser automated trust score for gowalter.co
  came back "Very Likely Safe" with a moderate score (I did not weight this heavily; automated
  scam-checkers are a weak signal, included only for completeness)
  [scamadviser.com/check-website/gowalter.co]. Absence of scandal is not the same as a verified
  track record.
- **Client logo banner (Airbus, Uber, Deloitte, Unilever, Facebook, IBM, NASA) is unverified** and
  reads like generic trust-signal marketing rather than confirmed direct placements; do not repeat
  these names as if Walter placed people at these companies.
  - **Posting freshness/pipeline concern**: duplicate shortcodes for the same title and
  duplicated, multi-country versions of many other roles on the same board suggest this could be
  an evergreen sourcing listing rather than a single funded opening, which affects how much
  urgency to apply with.

## 10. Cover letter angle: what to lead with, what to avoid

Given the above, there is very little genuine "why this company" material, because Walter is not
the eventual employer and has almost no distinctive mission/product narrative to lean into (its
narrative is entirely about being a recruiting middleman, not about the AI product Santiago would
actually build). The honest, defensible angle for any application text here is:

- **Lead with the role itself, not company narrative.** Speak directly to the job's actual ask,
  end-to-end ownership of production generative-AI features across a full stack, moving from
  architecture to shipped, maintainable systems, because that is concretely evidenced across
  Eugenia.ai (0-to-1 full-stack build to acquisition), Online Basics (LLM workflow + internal
  tooling that ran an $8M business), and Pragma AI (RAG "company brain" in production with eval
  harness and citation gating). This is the one hook that is genuine and specific to what the
  posting is actually asking for.
- **Do not write a "why Walter" paragraph that praises Walter as a company or its mission**, since
  Walter is not who Santiago would work for or with day to day; that would misrepresent the
  relationship and read as generic besides.
- **Avoid over-committing before pay is discussed.** Given the case-study signal that this
  channel's placements likely land well under his floor, keep early communication (if he applies)
  focused on establishing budget/employer identity before writing anything that assumes this is a
  fully vetted, well-funded role.
