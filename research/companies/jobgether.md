# Jobgether

Draft dossier for Santiago's review. Researched: 2026-08-24.

## 0. IMPORTANT: what Jobgether actually is for this application

Jobgether is a remote-job matching platform, not the employer. It aggregates and posts roles
"on behalf of a partner company, who manages all applications and next steps"
([Jobgether job posting language, matches job id 21118 description](https://www.adzuna.com.mx/details/5851152563)).
Jobgether itself confirms this flow on its own site: candidates are AI-matched and a shortlist is
handed to the hiring company, which runs interviews and makes the decision
([Jobgether, "Is Jobgether Legit"](https://jobgether.com/blog/is-jobgether-legit)).

For job id 21118 ("Forward Deployed Engineer, AI & Analytics", Mexico), the real employer is very
likely **Virtasant** (operating partly under its acquired brand **Gigster**), based on matching the
exact role title and near-identical description language to Virtasant's own careers page. This is
a strong inference, not a fully closed loop. See section 3 for the evidence and the one unresolved
discrepancy (a US-only eligibility clause on Virtasant's own posting of the same role).

**Practical implication for Santiago:** lean the cover letter and any "why this company" answer on
the actual work described in the posting (taking an AI/analytics proof of concept to
production-grade, embedding with a client, Palantir Foundry if confirmed) and, if he can verify
Virtasant/Gigster as the employer before applying, on Virtasant's outcome-based consulting model.
Do not build the narrative around Jobgether itself. Verify the employer identity directly with the
recruiter early (this is standard for Jobgether-routed roles) before finalizing any employer-specific
claims in a cover letter.

## 1. Snapshot

Jobgether: an AI-powered remote-job matching platform for senior professionals, founded 2020,
headquartered in Brussels, Belgium, roughly 11-50 employees, raised a EUR 900K pre-seed (July 2021)
and a EUR 1.4M seed (March 2023) from 4Ventures and finance.brussels
([Crunchbase funding round](https://www.crunchbase.com/funding_round/jobgether-seed--f66490d8),
[FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2023/03/jobgether-raises-e1-4m-in-seed-funding.html),
[LinkedIn company page](https://be.linkedin.com/company/jobgether)). It is a job-discovery and
matching layer, not the entity that would employ Santiago for this specific role.

## 2. Core narrative

Jobgether's own pitch is that traditional ATS keyword filters wrongly screen out experienced,
non-linear-career senior professionals from remote roles, and it exists to give them "the right
visibility in the right places" via AI matching and profile coaching rather than mass-apply volume
([Jobgether LinkedIn/about summary](https://be.linkedin.com/company/jobgether)). That narrative is
about Jobgether as a career-navigation product for job seekers; it is not the employer's narrative
Santiago would be joining, since Jobgether is only the distribution layer for this specific posting.

## 3. Mission & product

- Jobgether describes itself as "the largest remote job platform," aggregating 200,000+ remote
  listings from tens of thousands of companies, with AI CV/profile review and a "Match Score"
  system pairing candidates to roles ([jobgether.com search summary](https://jobgether.com/),
  [How Jobgether Works](https://jobgether.com/how-jobgether-works)).
- Revenue model leans on candidate-side subscriptions/premium coaching (profile optimization, AI
  interview prep) alongside free aggregated listings; a Jobgether blog post pushing back on "scam"
  claims states it does not sell user data and does not fabricate listings
  ([Is Jobgether Legit?](https://jobgether.com/blog/is-jobgether-legit)).
- Founded May 2020 by Juan Bourgois, Arnaud Devigne, Alex Hernandez, and Alexis Rodriguez
  ([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/funding_round/jobgether-seed--f66490d8)).

**Likely actual employer for this role, Virtasant / Gigster:**
- Virtasant is an Austin, Texas-based technology services company (cloud optimization, migration,
  managed cloud, custom "Build in Cloud" development), claiming an outcome-based billing model
  ("we don't bill you for software seats, hours of work, or deliverables, we get paid on outcomes")
  and a global distributed talent network ([virtasant.com](https://www.virtasant.com/)).
- Virtasant acquired Gigster (an on-demand software-development talent network) from Ionic Partners
  in March 2024; Gigster now operates under the Virtasant brand out of Austin
  ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/the-wire/ionic-partners-completes-sale-of-gigster-to-virtasant/),
  [Ionic Partners](https://ionicpartners.com/blog/ionic-partners-completes-sale-of-gigster-to-virtasant/)).
- Virtasant's own careers page lists an identical-titled role, "Forward Deployed Engineer, AI &
  Analytics," describing the work as taking a client's **Palantir Foundry** proof of concept
  (analytics/predictive) to production: data pipelines, ontology, application layer, governance,
  observability, training the client's engineers, and owning adoption metrics
  ([Virtasant Teamtailor posting](https://virtasant.teamtailor.com/jobs/8237846-forward-deployed-engineer-ai-analytics)).
  Public commentary on that listing identifies the end client as "a leading US healthcare workforce
  company" assembling a 5-6 person delivery pod (this detail comes from third-party summary of the
  Virtasant posting, not a primary Virtasant statement Santiago should quote directly; verify with
  the recruiter before repeating it).
- Virtasant also posts other remote-Latin-America roles through Jobgether under its own name and
  under the Gigster brand (e.g. "Business Analyst (remote, Latin America) at Virtasant," and
  several "at Gigster" listings), confirming Virtasant is an active Jobgether client that recruits
  into Mexico/LatAm ([Jobgether: Business Analyst, remote LatAm, Virtasant](https://jobgether.com/offer/69f08c51c646310ee39d3acc-business-analyst-remote-latin-america)).
- **Open discrepancy:** Virtasant's own Teamtailor posting for this exact title restricts
  eligibility to "contiguous US states," US citizens/Green Card holders/LLC sole proprietors, no
  visa sponsorship. The job in Santiago's pipeline (id 21118, via Adzuna/Jobgether) is explicitly
  "based in Mexico." This could mean Virtasant runs a parallel Mexico/LatAm-targeted requisition
  for the same client engagement, or it could mean the Mexico posting is a different, unrelated
  client using the same job-title template (this title/description pattern for "Forward Deployed
  Engineer, AI & Analytics" tied to Palantir Foundry work is a common template across several
  companies found in search: Virtasant, Automation Anywhere, Databricks, OneStream, Caylent, etc.
  each have their own "Forward Deployed Engineer" postings). Santiago should confirm the employer
  name and eligibility directly with whoever contacts him post-application before assuming
  Virtasant is definite.

  blog content addressing its own Trustpilot criticism directly and somewhat defensively
  ([Is Jobgether Legit?](https://jobgether.com/blog/is-jobgether-legit)). Trustpilot shows 4.3/5
  across 458 reviews, but a search summary flags some review-authenticity concerns worth knowing
  about if Santiago name-checks Jobgether at all (he should not; see section 0)
  ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobgether.com)).
- Virtasant: markets an "outcome-based," partner-not-vendor tone, and its careers page states it
  wants to "have fun" building "the next generation technology services company"
  ([virtasant.com](https://www.virtasant.com/)). No engineering blog or deep culture content was
  found; Virtasant reads as a consulting/managed-services shop rather than a product company with
  a public engineering voice.

## 7. Why-fit angles for Santiago

Do not build the pitch around Jobgether (it is the platform, not the employer). Build it around the
work described in the posting, and, if confirmed, Virtasant/Gigster's client-embedded delivery model.

- **Best hook:** Santiago has repeatedly taken an AI/analytics proof of concept to a
  production-grade system single-handedly or as the lead builder: Pragma AI's "company brain" RAG
  system (scoped retrieval, citation gating, eval harness) went from prototype to a client-facing
  productized offering; Online Basics' internal LLM workflow and data pipelines went from a
  scrappy tool to running an $8M/year operation. This maps directly onto the posting's core ask
  ("take a proof of concept into a production-grade platform with measurable business impact,"
  per job id 21118's description) more literally than almost any other role in his pipeline.
- **Second hook:** the posting wants someone who "embeds with the client to understand their
  processes and translate them into scalable technical solutions." Santiago's founder history is
  entirely client-facing, on-site deployments at Eugenia.ai (onboarding HOAs and property admins,
  translating conflicting requirements into shipped product), and Pragma AI's client diagnostics
  and onboarding work. This is the Family B (deployment/enablement) fit his profile explicitly
  prioritizes.
- **Honest concerns / watch-outs:**
  - No direct Palantir Foundry experience on his resume. If Virtasant/Foundry is confirmed as the
    real stack, this is a real gap to name plainly rather than paper over; his AI/RAG/LLM-agent
    experience is transferable but Foundry-specific tooling (Pipeline Builder, Ontology, Workshop)
    is not something he can claim hands-on.
  - Employer identity is unconfirmed. Santiago should not assert "Virtasant" as fact in a cover
    letter unless he verifies it (e.g. the confirmation email, recruiter call, or an explicit
    company name at application time). If it turns out to be a different client entirely, the
    Palantir Foundry framing above would not apply and the letter should stay generic to the
    posting's own language instead.
  - If Virtasant is the employer, note the parallel US-only-eligibility posting for the identical
    title; worth a direct, early question to the recruiter about Mexico eligibility and comp
    structure (likely contractor, not employee, per Santiago's own comp.contractor_note) rather
    than assuming parity with the US listing.

## 8. Application-question angles

- **Why this company:** lead with the nature of the work, not Jobgether. "The posting describes
  taking an AI and analytics proof of concept into a production-grade platform while embedded with
  the client, that is close to the exact motion I've run twice as a founder: turning a rough LLM
  workflow into the system that ran an 8 million dollar business, and turning a RAG prototype into
  Pragma AI's flagship client offering." If the employer is confirmed as Virtasant/Gigster, add a
  sentence on their outcome-based, client-embedded delivery model as a natural fit for someone who
  has always owned outcomes rather than tickets.
- **Why you fit:** anchor on the proof-of-concept-to-production arc (Pragma Brain, Online Basics
  internal platform) plus the client-embedding history (Eugenia.ai on-site deployments). Keep it to
  one concrete story, not a checklist of the whole resume.
  do not claim Foundry experience; if asked directly, name it as the one gap and pivot to the
  adjacent, real experience (RAG pipelines, LLM agent tooling, production data pipelines).
- **Anything else:** flag, briefly and non-defensively, that the posting came through Jobgether on
  behalf of an unnamed partner company, and that he would appreciate clarity early on the actual
  employer, Mexico-based eligibility, and whether the arrangement is contractor or employee, so he
  can speak concretely to the right team and comp structure.
