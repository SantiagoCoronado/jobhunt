## 1. Snapshot

Sticker Mule is a custom-printing company (stickers, labels, magnets, apparel, and more) that has
expanded into a suite of SaaS business tools under the "Sticker Mule Pro" brand. It is privately
owned (sole owner and CEO Anthony Constantino, no outside investors), profitable, and reports
"9-figures in annual revenue" [Ashby posting API]. Company-reported size (Feb 2026): "1,200+
employees across 30+ countries," with factories in Amsterdam NY, Gaffney SC, and Pisa, Italy
[globenewswire.com Feb 11, 2026 press release]. Founded 2010, headquartered in Amsterdam, New York
[globenewswire.com; stickermule.com/about]. No external funding: it explicitly markets itself as
running "with no meddlesome investors" [Ashby posting API].

## 2. Core narrative

Sticker Mule's distinctive story is a profitable, bootstrapped physical-manufacturing company
that reinvested its own cash into building software (and its own machines) rather than raising
venture capital, and is now trying to turn that software into a second product line: "Sticker Mule
Pro," a bundle of small-business tools (Stores, Notify, Reply, Hire Me, Write, Ship, Give,
Commissions) explicitly positioned as cheaper alternatives to Shopify, Mailchimp, Zendesk, and
Contra [stickermule.com/pro; Ashby posting API]. The company pairs that build-it-ourselves
instinct with an unusually loud, stunt-driven marketing style, most visibly its February 2026
"Multi-Million Dollar Giveback Initiative," a cash-giveaway campaign fronted by social-media
personality Greatness in a gold Rolls-Royce that generated 13M+ views in 24 hours
[globenewswire.com Feb 10-11, 2026 press releases]. The same founder-controlled structure that
lets Sticker Mule move this fast and fund this kind of marketing out of its own profits also means
CEO Anthony Constantino's personal political activity (a mass customer email endorsing Trump in
2024, a 2025 Congressional run) has repeatedly become the company's brand story, with no board or
outside investor to separate the two [forbes.com; legalclarity.org; dailygazette.com].

## 3. Mission & product

- Core printing business: custom stickers, labels, magnets, decals, and (per a February 2025
  launch) fabric stickers, a new substrate "never been sold before," letting customers print
  stickers on laptops, water bottles, and backpacks [globenewswire.com, Feb 18, 2025].
- Sticker Mule Pro ($9/month subscription, 2,882 reported current users) bundles small-business
  software tools: Stores (a Shopify-style storefront, "set up a store in minutes, add products,
  and start selling"), Studio (design tool), Notify (email/SMS campaigns), Reply (per the Pro
  page, "manage X queries with your team"; per the target job posting, positioned as a "Zendesk
  alternative" for support), Write (blogging), Ship (UPS shipping labels), Give (giveaways/email
  list building, launched officially July 2025), Events, and Commissions (10% referral payouts,
  launched January 22, 2026, which paid out $122,000 during its beta) [stickermule.com/pro;
  globenewswire.com Jan 22, 2026].
- Hire Me is a live marketplace letting customers hire creators directly for flat-rate Design
  Services and Video Services through a store, positioned in the target posting as a "Contra
  alternative" [stickermule.com/support/faq/hire-me; Ashby posting API]. It shipped team purchasing
  and revision workflows in the week of June 29 to July 5, 2026 [facebook.com/stickermule product
  update post].
- December 2025: completed its first acquisition of a direct competitor, Texas-based sticker
  printer Rockin Monkey; CEO framing was explicitly about keeping small competitors out of private
  equity roll-ups: Sticker Mule decided "it best to save their customers from private equity hell"
  [dailygazette.com; graphics-pro.com].
- Customers: described broadly as "creators and businesses"; celebrity/high-profile users of
  Stores have included Mike Tyson and Donald Trump Jr., per a December 2024 Forbes profile
  [forbes.com].

## 4. Culture & voice

- Stated hiring values, from Sticker Mule's own blog: they "hire for two things: skill and
  personality," built around five named traits: Humble ("People fail when the disparity between
  what they can do and what they think they can do becomes too vast"), Independent, Logical
  ("Success is the result of the sum of our decisions and actions"), Thoughtful, and Autodidactic.
  They state they will reject a skilled candidate on personality misfit, and frame this as more
  important, not less, for a remote team: "Culture is even more important when you're remote"
  [stickermule.com/blog/characteristics-we-look-for-in-team-members; stickermule.com/careers].
- Public voice is irreverent and stunt-driven: the February 2026 Giveback Initiative (cash handed
  out from a gold Rolls-Royce, fronted by an influencer with 20M+ followers) is the clearest
  example, generating over 13 million cross-platform views in its first 24 hours and reportedly
  more than doubling Google searches for "Sticker Mule" [globenewswire.com Feb 10 and 11, 2026].
- Benefits/rewards language leans "above-market, no excuses": "we monitor industry salaries and
  pay above market compensation," plus signing bonuses "to show our commitment to your success
  before you start," standing desks/ergonomic chairs on request, and 4 weeks vacation plus
  country-specific holidays [stickermule.com/careers; Ashby posting API].
- Employee-review signal is mixed and worth naming honestly: aggregated Glassdoor data surfaced in
  search results (not independently re-verifiable here; Glassdoor blocked direct fetching with a
  403) put the overall rating around 2.6/5 with roughly a third of reviewers recommending the
  company to a friend, and cited complaints about cross-department communication and generic
  rejection emails [glassdoor.com Sticker Mule overview and interview pages, via aggregated search
  results, low confidence]. Treat this as directional, not confirmed firsthand.

## 6. Engineering & AI direction

- Current stated stack (from the live target posting): "Ship across web and mobile from the same
  codebase. We use Go, TypeScript, React, Expo, GraphQL, Postgres, and run on GCP" [Ashby posting
  API]. Requirements are deliberately generalist and AI-forward: "You are an exceptional full-stack
  software engineer" and "You use AI aggressively and write well in English," with an explicit
  operating principle to "Use AI aggressively to push the limit of what we can do" [Ashby posting
  API].
- Working model: "Small, autonomous teams that own products end-to-end," prioritizing "what
  matters right now" over long-term roadmaps, and an explicit bias to "Move fast, build for the
  masses, and deprioritize edge cases" [Ashby posting API]. Team is fully async, remote-first,
  coordinating over Asana and Slack, with no daily-standup or office-hours culture implied by any
  source found.
- Historical engineering context (dated, useful for evolution but not current-state): a 2015
  Underdog.io writeup described a Ruby on Rails (Spree) monolith with MySQL, mid-migration to
  CoreOS/Docker on AWS and starting to extract microservices [underdog.io, Sept 2, 2015]. A 2018
  GraphQL Guide interview with engineers David North and Marco Lisci described a GraphQL API
  gateway (Node.js, express-graphql, Apollo Client) fronting gRPC-based backend services, adopted
  roughly a year before that October 2018 publication [medium.com/graphql-guide, Oct 2018]. Both
  predate the current Go/TypeScript/GCP stack described in the live posting and should be read as
  "how the engineering culture evolved," not as today's stack.
- A third-party tech-stack aggregator (Himalayas) lists a broader historical toolset (Node.js,
  Rails, Next.js, .NET, Python, C#, Ruby, Kubernetes, Docker, PostgreSQL, Redis, Kustomer,
  Contentful) that appears to blend current and legacy tools without dating them; treat as
  lower-confidence background color, not an authoritative current stack
  [himalayas.app/companies/sticker-mule/tech-stack].
- AI direction is stated as a working principle rather than a specific tool investment: aggressive,
  practitioner-level AI usage is a stated requirement for the hire, and "AI tools (internal and
  customer-facing)" is explicitly listed as one of the six product areas a new Software Engineer
  will help build and scale [Ashby posting API].

## 7. Hiring process (what is actually known, and what is not)

Sticker Mule is known for treating hiring as a personality filter first, skill filter second, per
its own stated philosophy above, and for using open-ended, research-requiring application
questions rather than a plain resume upload: aggregated candidate reports describe an online
application with short-answer questions that require reading the company's own site first,
sometimes followed by a short work-sample test (one reported example: drafting three sample
customer-service emails to test written English), then a video interview [indeed.com Sticker Mule
hiring-process FAQ and interview pages; interviewprep.org, aggregated secondary source]. Reported
average time-to-decision across all roles is about 9 days, based on a small sample of 64
self-reported Glassdoor interviews; some candidates report generic rejection emails and a
consolation $50 site credit for unsuccessful applicants [glassdoor.com Sticker Mule interview page,
via aggregated search results, low confidence, not independently re-fetched]. No 2024 to 2026,
software-engineer-specific interview account was found; treat the above as directional only and
expect it may differ for this specific evergreen "exceptional full-stack engineer" posting
(live since at least July 28, 2025, per its Ashby `publishedAt` timestamp, suggesting this is a
standing, always-open req rather than a one-off hire).
