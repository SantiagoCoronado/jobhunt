# EasyAudit AI, Inc.

*Draft dossier for Santiago's review. All claims are sourced below; items without a solid public
source are explicitly marked "unverified." This is a very small, early-stage startup, so public
information is thin and sometimes conflicting (see notes on HQ and funding). Refreshed

## 1. Snapshot

EasyAudit is an early-stage, AI-native compliance automation platform (agentic SaaS) that helps
lean startups and SMBs achieve and maintain SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, HIPAA, GDPR, and other
security/AI-governance certifications, replacing manual consultants and legacy GRC dashboards with
AI agents that write policies, map controls, and collect evidence. Founded in 2023 to 2024 by
Christian Khoury (CEO, ex-Deloitte risk and compliance) and Aidan Slack-Watkins. Small but growing
team: 6 employees (May 2026) to 7 (Tracxn, as of June 30, 2026) to 9 (LinkedIn, as read
2026-08-11); treat the exact current number as a moving, roughly single-digit figure, not a fixed
count. HQ is reported inconsistently: Toronto/Canada in some sources (including an "Canadian
Innovation Week" spotlight post) versus Miami, FL, US on the company LinkedIn page; still
unresolved. Fully remote hiring, worldwide, confirmed on both live job postings. Funding remains
undisclosed at a specific amount: aggregator data shows one round involving RevTech Labs (a
Charlotte, NC accelerator/fund), with figures conflicting across sources (as low as ~$20K per
PitchBook-derived data, versus a ~$200K pre-seed reported around October 2025 in company LinkedIn
content); no new funding announcement was found since the July 2026 research pass. [Sources: 1,
2, 4, 5, 14]

## 2. Core narrative

EasyAudit's pitch is "compliance without the compliance tax": a team of specialized AI agents
(not a checklist UI) that does the actual work of writing policies, mapping controls, and
collecting evidence, so a 3-person SaaS startup can pass a clean SOC 2 audit in about three weeks
with under five hours of internal effort, instead of hiring a compliance officer or consultant.
The founder-market fit story is direct: an ex-Deloitte compliance analyst who saw founders
"duct-taping Google Docs, overpriced platforms, and part-time consultants" to fake being
audit-ready, and built the AI-agent alternative. Current careers copy states the company has
"product-market fit" and is "pouring fuel on the fire," a confidence signal worth noting even
though it is the company's own framing, not an independently verified metric. They frame the
market as moving from "Era 2" dashboard-and-checklist compliance tools (Vanta, Drata,
Secureframe) to "Era 3," where compliance becomes an invisible, continuously-running background
function. [Sources: 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 14]

## 3. Mission & product

- **What they build**: an agentic compliance platform. AI agents generate tailored security
  policies and a risk register from a company's actual tech stack, map existing docs/evidence to
  required controls, connect to systems (AWS, GCP, Azure, GitHub, Notion, Okta, Slack, Microsoft
  365) to auto-collect evidence, and prepare audit-ready documentation; a partnered CPA firm signs
  off on the resulting SOC 2 report. Frameworks covered: SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001 (AI
  management systems), HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA, NIST CSF, CMMC. [Sources: 1, 6, 9]
- **Business model**: B2B SaaS, flat/transparent pricing positioned explicitly against Vanta,
  Drata, Sprinto, and Secureframe ("no hidden fees or upsells"). [Source: 1]
- **Customers**: lean, early-stage SaaS companies and founders (often pre-compliance-hire) who
  need a certification to unblock enterprise sales cycles. Documented customers: Alignd (3-person
  SaaS startup, Burlington VT, hit SOC 2 Type I in 3 weeks with zero exceptions) and TheStage AI
  (SOC 2 achieved, per company LinkedIn posts). [Sources: 6, 10]
- **Differentiation claim**: EasyAudit publishes an analysis claiming its customers' SOC 2 reports
  average ~40% textual similarity to each other (implying real customization), versus ~99.8% for a
  named competitor, Delve, whom they characterize as producing "templates" rather than genuine
  programs. Treat this as a company-authored marketing claim, not independently verified. [Source: 10]
- **Continued content push**: released a roughly 90-second brand film (posted to LinkedIn around
  late July 2026) framing EasyAudit as the alternative to handing an employee SOC 2/HIPAA/ISO/NIST
  work on top of their real job; consistent with the existing "compliance without the compliance
  tax" narrative rather than a new direction. [Source: 5]

## 4. Culture & voice

- Public-facing voice is direct, founder-to-founder, and slightly combative toward incumbent GRC
  vendors ("compliance shouldn't kill your startup," "no compliance tax"). Marketing leans on
  concrete before/after metrics (3 weeks, under 5 hours, zero exceptions) rather than enterprise
  GRC jargon. [Sources: 6, 7]
- Stated values (company About page): Innovation, User-Centricity, Integrity, Excellence.
  [Source: 3]

  need a manager, a sprint cadence, and three layers of process to function, this is not for
  you." Feedback style is described as "direct, and on our hours... no politics, no corporate

  (Full-Stack req only; the GTM req lists "competitive salary" without an explicit equity line),
  "individual voice" and growth as the company scales, described as "mission-driven." [Sources:
  2, 14]
- Organizational signal: the GTM Engineer role reports to CEO Christian Khoury and works closely
  with a Chief Revenue Officer (CRO), confirming a CRO function now exists even though no public

- Team is still tiny (single digits), so culture is closer to "early founding team" than "company

## 6. Engineering & AI direction

- **Core-product stack (Full-Stack Developer posting)**: Frontend in JavaScript/TypeScript,
  React.js, Next.js, MUI; backend in Node.js and Python; testing with Playwright and Storybook;
  Postgres and S3 for data/storage; deployed across Azure, AWS, GCP, Vercel, Render, and Supabase.
  AI layer built on the OpenAI Agents SDK and Google's A2A (agent-to-agent) protocol, with the team
  itself using ChatGPT, Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex as AI-assisted dev tools. [Source: 2]
- **Separate growth-engineering stack (GTM Engineer posting)**: JavaScript/TypeScript, Tailwind,
  Python, PostgreSQL, with OpenAI, ElevenLabs, and fal.ai for AI content generation, plus Meta
  Marketing API, Slack API, and Attio CRM API integrations. The posting is explicit that this role
  "will not touch our core platform, that's our internal engineering team's domain" and owns
  "standalone tools, dashboards, integrations, and automations" instead. This confirms the two
  reqs sit in genuinely separate parts of the org (core product engineering vs. growth
  engineering), not two postings for the same seat. [Source: 14]
- **Separately posted Senior Backend Developer role** skews toward Go and Python for "agentic AI
  engineering" across AWS/Azure/GCP, remote US/Canada; the timing of this posting is inconsistently
  dated across sources (one read puts it around March 2026, a later read around June 2026), so
  treat it as an existing, ongoing backend req rather than a single dated event. Suggests the
  backend may be diversifying beyond Node, or that different services use different languages.
  [Source: 5, 11]
- **AI approach**: multi-agent architecture ("a team of AI agents," not a single chatbot) that
  performs discrete jobs (policy generation, control mapping, evidence collection, gap analysis,
  audit-doc prep) rather than one monolithic assistant. No public technical writeup on model
  choice, RAG design, or evaluation approach was found; the "AI-native" and "world's first
  AI-native platform" claims are company marketing, not independently verified. [Sources: 1, 9]
- No dedicated public engineering blog was found; engineering practice (testing rigor, deploy
  cadence, on-call, etc.) is not independently documented beyond the job postings themselves.

## 7. Why-fit angles for Santiago

**Best hook to lead with (for the Full-Stack Developer role, the live process)**: Santiago has
personally built the exact category of product EasyAudit sells (an AI agent that ingests a
company's real data and does the work a human specialist used to do) as a founder shipping to real
B2B clients under deadline pressure, not just as an engineer following a spec. Pragma AI's
"company brain" (RAG over internal company data to answer questions and auto-generate business
reports) is structurally the same problem EasyAudit solves for compliance evidence and policy
generation.

- **Agent-native stack overlap**: the Full-Stack role asks for OpenAI Agents SDK, tool-use/
  function-calling AI agents, React/Next.js, Node/Python, Postgres, AWS. Santiago's master resume
  shows direct, recent hands-on work with exactly this shape of stack (Claude Agent SDK, MCP tool
  layers, agent frameworks with function calling, FastAPI/Python, React/TypeScript/Vite,
  PostgreSQL, AWS, Supabase) across Plutus, Kairos, and the Pragma AI personal-finance agent.
- **Founder who ships 0-to-1 alone or in tiny teams**: EasyAudit is a single-digit-headcount team
  that explicitly wants someone who "gets features from local to staging without constant
  oversight" in a "fast-paced" startup where "priorities shift rapidly," and, per the newer GTM
  posting, a team that has no patience for "a manager, a sprint cadence, and three layers of
  process." Santiago founded and ran two companies (Eugenia.ai to acquisition, Online Basics to
  $8M revenue on a 7-person team) and is currently building solo AI products end to end (Plutus,
  Kairos).
- **Trust/verification framing**: EasyAudit's core value prop is AI output a CPA and an enterprise
  buyer will actually trust. Santiago's LLM work (Online Basics: custom prompt engineering plus a
  multi-stage validation pipeline for AI-generated content) is directly relevant experience
  building guardrails around AI output that a human reviews before it goes out the door.

**On the second req, GTM Engineer (local id 6909, matcher score 87)**: this is a genuinely
different seat from the one Santiago is interviewing for, not a duplicate. It reports to the CEO
and CRO instead of engineering, explicitly excludes the core platform, and is scoped to marketing/
sales tooling (CRM automation, ad dashboards, outbound systems). The skill overlap is real

scored well, but three things argue for caution before pursuing it as a second, parallel track:

- **The posting is closed.** As of 2026-08-11 it is marked "no longer accepting applications" and
  the company's Get on Board profile shows no open roles. There is no live public application path
  for it right now.
- **Pay clears his floor only in the upper half of the band.** The GTM req's posted band ($4,200
  to $6,900/month) annualizes to roughly $50,400 to $82,800, so it clears his current $70,000
  LATAM-scope floor only above about $5,850/month. Worth flagging: the Full-Stack req he is
  already interviewing for is in the same shape, its posted band ($5,000 to $7,000/month)
  annualizes to $60,000 to $84,000 and crosses the $70,000 floor (set 2026-08-25) at that same
  ~$5,850/month point. Neither band is a dealbreaker on its own for a role this far along or this
  well matched, but it is worth a direct comp conversation before any offer stage, on whichever
  req is in play.
- **Two live threads at one small company is a real judgment call.** Raising interest in a second,
  closed req mid-process risks reading as scattered to a 7-person team that just told him "this is
  not a product engineering role" for the other seat. If he wants to pursue it, the natural path is
  a direct, low-key note to his existing contact (Brooke or Reuben) expressing interest once the
  Full-Stack decision lands, not a fresh cold application, since there is no form to fill out
  anyway.

**Honest concerns / watch-outs**:
- **Compliance domain is new to him.** Nothing in his resume shows SOC 2/ISO 27001/GRC/audit
  experience. The Full-Stack posting lists compliance background as "desirable," not required, so
  this is a gap to name plainly rather than paper over.
- **Very small, thinly documented company.** Headcount is roughly 6 to 9 people depending on
  source and date, funding is small and unclearly disclosed, HQ location is inconsistent across
  sources (Toronto/Canada vs Miami, FL), and there is no public engineering blog. Real seed/
  pre-seed risk: limited runway visibility, no independent evidence of what engineering process/
  on-call looks like day to day.
- **Pay band is modest for his level and below his current floor**, as detailed above for both
  reqs. Fine if equity and mission compensate, worth a candid gut-check and a direct question in
  process.
- **Backend/stack drift across reqs.** Full-Stack is Node/Python/React; the Senior Backend req
  skews Go/Python; GTM Engineering is a JS/TS/Tailwind/Python stack disconnected from the core
  platform. Worth clarifying which stack he would actually touch day to day if he gets an offer.
- **Remote timezone expectation differs by req.** The GTM posting explicitly requires US Eastern
  business-hours overlap (9am to 5pm ET, at least core hours), which from Mexico City (currently
  UTC-6 vs. Eastern's UTC-4 during daylight time) means calls from roughly 7am to 3pm local, an

  requirement, though some real-time overlap should be assumed given the interview process is
  happening live with a US-based CTO contact.
