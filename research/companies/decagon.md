## 1. Snapshot

Decagon AI, Inc., San Francisco (also New York and London). Private, growth-stage AI company
founded August 2023 by Jesse Zhang (CEO) and Ashwin Sreenivas (CTO). Builds conversational AI
agents ("the AI concierge for every customer") for enterprise customer service across voice,
chat, email, and SMS. Raised roughly $481 million to $500 million total across Series A through
D; latest round (Series D, January 27, 2026) was $250 million at a $4.5 billion valuation, up
from $1.5 billion six months earlier. Estimated ARR reached about $35 million by October 2025, up
from about $10 million at the end of 2024 (Sacra estimate, not an official Decagon figure).
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagon_(company)); [Sacra company profile](https://sacra.com/c/decagon/); [Forbes, Feb 6 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alexyork/2026/02/06/ai-agent-startup-decagon-triples-valuation-to-45-billion/); [Decagon, Series D announcement](https://decagon.ai/blog/series-d-announcement)

## 2. Core narrative

Decagon is racing to prove that AI agents, not just AI copilots, can run enterprise customer
service end to end, and it is doing it through a deliberately specialized, service-heavy go to
market: rather than shipping a self-serve product and hoping it works, Decagon builds an internal
"Agent Engineer" function that embeds with each customer to translate real workflows into working
agents, then splits that further (as of early 2026) into a dedicated "Agent Builder Org," a
specialized technical delivery team hired specifically to own hands-on build execution for
strategic enterprise accounts at scale. This is a company betting that deployment craft, not just
model quality, is the moat: over 80 percent of its model traffic already runs on models it trained
itself rather than foundation-model APIs, and its stated values ("Just Get It Done," "Winner's
Mindset") describe an in-office, high-velocity, founder-led culture rather than a research lab.
The role Santiago is looking at (Director of Customer Engineering, Agent Builder) is not a
generic hire, it is the leadership seat for the exact org Decagon stood up to solve the problem it
says is hardest: turning powerful models into agents that actually work in production for a
specific enterprise.
[Decagon, Introducing the Agent Engineer](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-agent-engineer); [Decagon, Introducing Decagon Labs](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-decagon-labs); [Job posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/decagon/3aa855e4-164b-4246-aaa0-b602339bfe06/application)

## 3. Mission & product

- **Product**: an AI agent platform for customer experience, built around three parts: Channels
  (voice, chat, email agents), Build (Agent Operating Procedures, natural-language workflow
  definitions customers or Decagon staff configure), and Optimize/Scale (testing, experimentation,
  analytics). [Decagon homepage](https://decagon.ai/)
- **Mission statement**: "Build, optimize, and scale AI agents that treat every customer like the
  only one." [Decagon homepage](https://decagon.ai/)
- **Business model**: enterprise SaaS, sold and delivered with heavy technical services
  (deployment, configuration, iteration) rather than pure self-serve.
  [Decagon, Introducing the Agent Engineer](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-agent-engineer)
- **Named customers**: Chime, Duolingo, ClassPass, Hunter Douglas, Oura, Curology, Valon,
  Rippling, Riot Games, Fanatics, Cash App/Block, Notion, Eventbrite, Substack, Avis Budget Group,
  Deutsche Telekom, Hertz, Bilt, Affirm; 100+ new enterprise logos added in 2025.
  [Decagon homepage](https://decagon.ai/); [Decagon, Series D announcement](https://decagon.ai/blog/series-d-announcement); [Microsoft for Startups blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/blog/decagon-building-the-ai-concierge-for-modern-customer-experience/)
- **Reported outcomes** (Decagon/Microsoft-sourced, treat as company-provided marketing figures
  rather than independently audited): average inquiry deflection approaching 70 percent across the
  customer base, Duolingo above 80 percent; Oura reported roughly 3x CSAT improvement; ClassPass
  reported a 95 percent drop in support-conversation cost.
  [Microsoft for Startups blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/blog/decagon-building-the-ai-concierge-for-modern-customer-experience/)

## 6. Engineering & AI direction

- **Model strategy**: deliberately moved away from depending on general-purpose foundation models
  toward "a network of specialized models, each responsible for a distinct function" (intent
  interpretation, workflow execution, hallucination detection, escalation decisions). States that
  over 80 percent of production model traffic runs on models Decagon trained itself, which it says
  outperform foundation models on its specific enterprise use cases.
  [Decagon, Introducing Decagon Labs](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-decagon-labs)
- **Fine-tuning approach**: combines supervised fine-tuning (curated input/output pairs, e.g.
  training a workflow-selection model on historical queries paired with the correct Agent Operating
  Procedure) with reinforcement learning (GRPO, GSPO, DPO) for more abstract behaviors, prioritizing
  accuracy first and low latency second, especially for voice.
  [Decagon, Engineering fast, performant AI agents through fine-tuning](https://decagon.ai/blog/fine-tuning-ai-agents)
- **Infrastructure**: hosts a diverse set of proprietary and third-party models on Microsoft Azure,
  using Azure AI Foundry to deploy fine-tuned models close to users for latency and data-residency
  reasons, across multiple regions, with safe rollout/rollback of model versions in production.
  [Microsoft for Startups blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/blog/decagon-building-the-ai-concierge-for-modern-customer-experience/)
- **Decagon Labs**: internal research and agent-orchestration arm focused on frontier post-training
  techniques, agent architectures, and voice agents specifically, with an explicit goal of turning
  research directly into production rather than pure publication.
  [Decagon, Introducing Decagon Labs](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-decagon-labs)
- **Delivery engineering (the function this role leads)**: the Agent Engineer / Agent Builder
  function does discovery and scoping embedded with customers, rapid prototyping of agentic systems
  over LLMs/APIs/the Decagon platform, prompt-chain and routing/fallback design, and production
  iteration (benchmarking latency, building retries/failover/monitoring), then feeds recurring
  customer needs back into the core platform roadmap rather than treating each deployment as a
  one-off. [Decagon, Introducing the Agent Engineer](https://decagon.ai/blog/introducing-agent-engineer); [Decagon, Inside Agent Engineering at Decagon](https://decagon.ai/blog/inside-agent-engineering-at-decagon)
- **Competitive landscape**: main named competitor is Sierra AI (founded 2024 by Bret Taylor and
  Clay Bavor), which closed a $950 million round at a $15.8 billion valuation in May 2026, roughly
  3.5x Decagon's valuation. Coverage frames the two companies' deployment philosophies as speed
  (Decagon) versus depth/governance (Sierra), a genuine strategic risk if enterprises increasingly
  value the latter. [Sacra, Decagon vs Sierra](https://sacra.com/research/decagon-vs-sierra/)

## 8. Why-fit angles for Santiago

**Lead hook**: Decagon just split out an entire new organization (Agent Builder) because it
concluded that shipping working enterprise AI agents is a distinct, hands-on delivery discipline,
not a byproduct of good models. Santiago has been running exactly that discipline solo and for
clients for years: owning full-lifecycle AI deployments (scoping ambiguous requirements, building
the system, iterating in production, feeding learnings back into the product) rather than either
pure research or pure account management. That is a precise match to what this Director role is
chartered to lead, not a generic "AI experience" claim.

Supporting angles:

- The role is explicitly about building the team, playbooks, and operating system for a delivery
  function, not just doing the delivery himself. Santiago has done this before at founder scale:
  led a 7-person team at Online Basics with git-based workflows, code review, and incident
  response; owned the full product lifecycle and on-site client deployments at Eugenia.ai
  (onboarding HOAs, translating conflicting stakeholder needs into shipped product); and is now
  co-building Pragma AI's own delivery playbooks (15 agent definitions, 10 reusable routines,
  onboarding diagnostics) for client AI consulting engagements.
- Decagon's "even better if" list calls out production LLM-agent deployment (prompting, evaluation,
  guardrails, tooling, workflow design) and enterprise SaaS integrations. Santiago can point to
  concrete, shipped systems: Pragma Brain's RAG "company brain" with citation gating and an
  automated eval harness, Plutus's MCP control plane and autonomous discovery agent, and Kairos's
  dual Claude Agent SDK / MCP integration over one typed tool layer, real evaluation and guardrail
  design experience, not just usage of AI tools.
- Decagon's stated culture (Just Get It Done, Winner's Mindset, in-office, high velocity) rewards
  someone comfortable with ambiguity and founder-style ownership, which matches a 3x founder profile
  more than it matches a large-company process manager.

**Honest concerns / watch-outs**:

- This role is explicitly in-office in San Francisco, not remote or hybrid-from-Mexico-City.
  Santiago is Mexico City based and TN-visa eligible for US employment, so the role is workable
  under his own location rule (US on-site is acceptable via TN, `config/profile.yaml`), but it is
  a real relocation decision, not a remote arrangement, and should be treated as such going in.
- The "10+ years" and "5+ years of people management" requirements are the biggest gap to be
  candid about: Santiago has founder-level ownership and has led a 7-person team, but that is a
  different shape of experience than 5+ years formally managing managers or senior ICs inside a
  larger organization. This should be addressed directly rather than glossed over.
- Decagon's own reported growth figures (deflection rates, CSAT gains, cost reductions) are
  company-sourced marketing claims, not independently audited; worth treating with normal
  skepticism in conversation rather than repeating them as verified fact.
- Competitive pressure is real: Sierra AI, the most directly comparable competitor, raised at
  roughly 3.5x Decagon's valuation four months after Decagon's own round, in a market moving very
  fast. This is a volatile, capital-intensive space rather than a settled one.
- In-office, high-velocity, "Winner's Mindset" culture as stated by the company itself is a signal
  worth taking literally: this is not a low-intensity role.

- **Why this company**: lead with the fact that Decagon just organizationally admitted something
  Santiago has believed and lived for years: that shipping AI agents that actually work in
  production is a distinct, hands-on craft, not a side effect of model quality. He is not drawn to
  Decagon because it is a hot AI company, he is drawn to it because it just built the exact
  function (Agent Builder) around the exact problem (translating enterprise complexity into working
  agents) that has been his actual job as a founder.
- **Why you fit**: anchor on the dual shape of the role (build the org AND drive the technical
  delivery), not a checklist of AI buzzwords. Point to one concrete build (Pragma Brain's eval
  harness and citation gating, or Kairos's MCP/Agent SDK integration) as proof of real agent-
  deployment craft, and one concrete leadership proof point (leading Online Basics' 7-person team,
  or owning Eugenia.ai's on-site client rollouts end to end) as proof he can build the operating
  system, not just do the work personally.
- **Anything else**: be direct and brief about the two real questions before this goes further:
  the in-office San Francisco relocation from Mexico City (workable via TN visa, and a decision he
  is prepared to make, but worth surfacing early rather than late), and the people-management
  depth question (founder-scale team leadership versus 5+ years managing managers in a larger org),
