# Quinncia (Quinncia Inc)

## Snapshot
AI career readiness platform for universities (resume analysis, mock interviews, LinkedIn feedback for students), founded 2015 by CEO Himal Ahuja, HQ Boston, small team (roughly 18 to 21 employees per LeadIQ and PitchBook, "11 to 50" per The Org), fully remote and distributed across continents, over 100 university/college customers, modest early funding (Wellfound lists a $160K round from 2015; other trackers cite roughly $1.4M to $1.6M raised total, sources conflict and no confirmed recent round).

## Core narrative
Quinncia is a bootstrapped-feeling, decade-old edtech company (founded 2015, still 18 to 21 people) that has quietly built an AI product now used at 100+ universities, including big public schools like Arizona State, University of Florida, and UConn, by staying narrowly focused on one problem: most students never get real feedback on their resume or interview skills before they apply for jobs. The founder's own origin story (strong degrees, no interviews, no idea why) is the company's north star, and the product literally trains against the same ATS and video-screening algorithms employers use, so students practice against the real filter, not a generic mock. It is a small, mission-driven team punching above its size in enterprise higher-ed sales, now investing in a frontend rebuild to catch up product-wise.

## Mission & product
- Core product is "Quinn," an AI assistant covering three modules: resume analysis (upload a resume, get ATS-style flags and formatting/content feedback in under 2 minutes), mock interview practice (evaluates verbal answers plus eye contact, speech rate, filler words, and body language), and LinkedIn profile feedback. (quinncia.io, career.uconn.edu)
- Business model: institutional licensing to universities and career-services centers ("flexible, budget-conscious pricing"); students at partner schools use it for free. Customers named publicly include Arizona State University, University of Florida, Texas Tech, Auburn, UConn, University of Rochester, MSU Denver, and Northern Illinois University. (quinncia.io, career.uconn.edu, msudenver.edu)
- Stated mission: "enhance student engagement and embed career readiness in both the classroom and student life," summarized as "Where Ability Meets Opportunity." (quinncia.io)
- Founder Himal Ahuja built the company after his own job search failed despite strong academic credentials, then saw the same gap from the hiring side. (medium.com/quinncia-blog, search results on Ahuja's background)

## Culture & voice
- Small, distributed team: LeadIQ and PitchBook both put headcount around 18 to 21 people across multiple continents (North America, Asia, Africa per one source); The Org lists CEO Himal Ahuja with 10 direct reports and a Boston HQ office of just 2 people, meaning most of the team is remote. (leadiq.com, pitchbook.com, theorg.com)
- Public tone is warm and mission-forward rather than flashy: language like "building doors for students and employers," client-facing values described as "flexible, approachable, patient, and extremely helpful." (quinncia.io)
- Recognition: named to a "top 50 edtech, emerging companies for higher education" list (2022) and part of the Lighthouse Labs cohort (2019). (quinncia.io)
- No dedicated engineering or product blog found; public writing is mostly founder-authored (Himal Ahuja on Medium) and higher-ed-outcomes focused rather than technical.

## Recent news
- November 6, 2025: University of Connecticut's Center for Career Readiness published a recap of a Quinncia product demo for staff, describing current features (resume analysis vs. six templates including three UConn-specific ones, unlimited resume uploads, capped LinkedIn analyses, interview practice tuned to resume and target industry) and explicitly cautioning that the tool should complement, not replace, human career coaching, especially for neurodivergent students. This is the most recent dated, verifiable Quinncia news found. (career.uconn.edu/blog/2025/11/06)
