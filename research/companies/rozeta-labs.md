# Rozeta Labs

Draft dossier for Santiago's review. Researched: 2026-08-11.

## 1. Snapshot

Early-stage AI implementation shop (self-described) that builds and operates custom AI agent
systems inside mid-market operational businesses, on a services model, not a software product.
Pennsylvania LLC incorporated February 27, 2026. Founder-run, tiny team (roughly 2-4 people
based on public listings). Fully remote, no office requirement found. No public funding
announcement found; appears founder-funded or bootstrapped. Compensation for the engineering
role researched here is quoted as a $4,000 to $5,000 USD/month independent-contractor rate.

## 2. Core narrative

Rozeta sells outcomes, not software: it embeds inside a client's operations, redesigns the
broken workflow, and ships production agent systems that survive contact with messy real-world
processes, explicitly positioning against "pilot-stage" AI vendors. The founder, Greg Van Horn,
frames it as "the AI company we'd want running our own businesses," built by people with
operations and P&L backgrounds rather than pure research engineers. It is a two-to-four-person
shop five months old, so the story is still mostly the founder's thesis and a handful of live
job postings rather than a track record with named clients.

## 3. Mission & product

- Mission, per Rozeta's own site: "to help companies scale faster by integrating AI agents into
  real operational workflows" (Torre.ai company profile, citing rozeta.ai copy).
- Product is a four-phase engagement: operational audit, architecture and redesign, ship to
  production, then operate and evolve, integrating with a client's existing CRMs, ERPs, finance
  tools, inboxes, and spreadsheets rather than requiring a rip-and-replace (rozeta.ai/about).
- Target customers are mid-market operational businesses: revenue and customer operations,
  finance and back office, field service operations, knowledge and reporting, compliance and
  risk, people and HR. Named example verticals from a job posting include home services,
  professional services, health and wellness, and private-equity-backed firms (Torre.ai,
  getonbrd postings).
- Explicit anti-positioning: "they don't sell software; they sell outcomes" (Torre.ai summary of
  rozeta.ai).

## 4. Culture & voice

- Four stated operating beliefs on rozeta.ai: workflows before automation (map the operational
  system first, coordination is usually the real bottleneck), production over pilots, no
  rip-and-replace, and economics must work (measurable payback tied to execution leverage)
  (rozeta.ai/about, via WebFetch summary).
- House tone is blunt, contrarian, and ROI-driven, visible in blog headline choices: "Why 73% of
  AI Pilots Never Reach Production," "The Mid-Market AI Trap: Why You Cannot Buy Your Way to
  Operational AI," "The CFO's Guide to AI Investment" (rozeta.ai/blog).
- Job postings for the engineering seat carry the same voice: a "you may not be a fit if" list
  that explicitly screens out people who "only want to work on model research," "mainly want to
  build demos," or "need perfectly written requirements before you can start" (getonbrd job
  6844 posting text).
- On Torre.ai, the company profile shows 824 people signaled interest, a 5-star rating across 10
  reviews, and roughly 1-hour candidate review speed, current team listed as Greg Van Horn
  (admin), Parinasan, and Axel Ross A., with two former members (Hassaan Zakria, Usman Shan) no
  longer listed. I could not verify why the former members left; flagging as an unverified
  churn signal at a very small team, not a confirmed problem.

## 6. Are the two postings the same role reposted? (the key question for this dossier)

Short answer: the evidence shows two distinct postings, not a duplicate or a simple repost, but
they overlap enough in remit, comp, and team size that Santiago should treat them as very likely
drawing from the same small hiring need rather than two independent headcount slots.

Evidence they are distinct postings:
- Different external IDs, canonical keys, and URLs in the job tracker (6982:
  `senior-agentic-ai-engineer-rozeta-labs-remote`; 6844:
  `applied-ai-engineer-agentic-systems-rozeta-labs-remote`).
- Different `posted_at` dates roughly 2.5 months apart (Mar 19 vs Jun 1, 2026), and both existed
  simultaneously in our first crawl (Jul 6, 2026), so there was a real window where both reqs
  were open at once, not one replacing the other outright.
- Materially different job description text. The Senior posting (confirmed word-for-word
  overlapping on a second board, Djinni) is short and titled around owning "every client
  engagement" next to the founder, explicitly requires Claude API and LangGraph/LangChain
  experience, C1 English, and a Loom video with the application. The Applied AI Engineer posting
  is a longer, more general "useful experience" and "great fit if / may not be a fit if" template
  with no seniority framing and no Loom requirement.
- The Senior posting's title and scope ("not a research, prompt engineering, or internal tooling
  role... you build production AI systems... you have done it before") reads more senior and
  more narrowly agent-engineering-focused than the Applied AI Engineer posting, which reads as a
  broader generalist screen.

Evidence they likely reflect the same underlying need rather than two separate growth hires:
- Identical published comp band on both postings: $4,000 to $5,000 USD/month.
- Same tiny team, same founder, same stage, no evidence of Rozeta having grown headcount or
  raised funding between the two postings.
- The Senior posting effectively went quiet (stopped being crawled, marked inactive on Djinni)
  in the weeks before the Applied AI Engineer posting became the only one still live, consistent
  with a company iterating on how to describe and staff one open engineering seat rather than
  running two concurrent searches to a finish.

Practical read: this is very likely one small company circling the same open engineering need
with two different job-posting framings, not confirmed duplicate listings and not confirmed
separate roles. Santiago already has a pending application (since 2026-07-22) against the Senior
posting. Applying again under the Applied AI Engineer posting risks reading as a second
application for the same underlying seat to a 2 to 4 person team that will very likely notice.
The lower-risk move is to follow up on the existing application (mention the Applied AI Engineer
posting if a broader/different scope is confirmed on a call) rather than submit a second,
separate application.

## 7. Engineering & AI direction

Sourced from the job postings (both the getonbrd listings and the Djinni mirror), since no
public engineering blog exists yet:
- Languages: TypeScript and/or Python.
- LLM APIs: OpenAI, Anthropic (Claude API named specifically on the Senior posting), Gemini.
- Agent stack: agent frameworks, workflow orchestration, structured outputs, tool calling,
  evals; the Senior posting names LangGraph/LangChain and daily use of Claude Code and Cursor.
- Retrieval: RAG pipelines, embeddings, vector databases, connected to client-specific
  knowledge.
- Integration surface: third-party APIs, webhooks, databases, CRMs, internal tools, SaaS
  platforms; building internal tools, dashboards, admin panels, approval flows, human review
  queues.
- Cloud: Cloudflare, AWS, GCP, Azure, Supabase, or Vercel (posting lists these as acceptable,
  not a fixed stack).
- Stated operating requirement: production AI needs guardrails, evals, logging, observability,
  permissioning, and human escalation, not just a working demo (getonbrd job 6844 text).
- Blog content (rozeta.ai/blog) argues for engineer-operator pairing over pure research teams,
  and for skipping extended pilots in favor of shipping directly into production with tight
  ROI accountability.

## 8. Why-fit angles for Santiago

Best hook to lead with: Santiago is not pitching potential, he is already running the same kind
of business Rozeta is trying to become. Pragma AI, his current co-founded consultancy, is a
client-facing AI implementation shop for SMBs: he runs discovery, designs the architecture,
writes the code, and keeps it in production for paying clients, which maps almost one-to-one
onto the Senior posting's "you are the technical engine behind every client engagement" framing
and onto Rozeta's stated belief in production over pilots.

Concrete matches:
- RAG pipeline over client-specific data: Pragma Brain (scoped retrieval, deterministic
  no-answer and citation gating, multilingual ES/EN embeddings, automated eval harness) is
  exactly the retrieval and evals work both postings ask for.
- Tool-use and CRM/business-system integration: Kairos exposes a CRM, tasks, and calendar
  through 46 typed tools via MCP to both an in-app agent and an MCP server; Pragma runs a
  hosted, authenticated MCP service over the company knowledge vault; Online Basics orchestrated
  10+ third-party marketplace and inventory APIs.
- Internal tools, dashboards, admin panels: named directly in the Applied AI Engineer posting's
  "useful experience" list and directly evidenced by Online Basics' internal pricing dashboard
  and inventory admin panel, used daily by operators.
- Operator-facing, messy-requirements work: 7 years as a founder translating ambiguous business
  needs into shipped systems (Eugenia.ai, Online Basics), which is precisely what both postings
  screen for ("you like turning vague business pain into working systems").
- Client-facing, US-facing English, TN-visa eligible for any US business travel; Mexico City is
  one hour off EST, a clean fit for the "async-first, EST overlap" requirement.

Honest concerns and watch-outs:
- Comp: the published $4,000-5,000 USD/month contractor rate annualizes to roughly $48,000 to
  $60,000, below Santiago's $70,000 floor for LATAM-scoped remote roles. Torre.ai lists "up to
  50% bonus" on top, which at the very top of the band ($5,000/month plus max bonus) would reach
  roughly $90,000, but that is a best case, not the base offer. This should be raised early
  rather than discovered late.
- Contractor, not employee: no confirmed benefits beyond a Torre.ai mention of Rozeta
  co-paying health insurance, which was not independently confirmed on rozeta.ai itself.
- Very small, very young team (five months old, no funding found, at least two people who no
  longer appear on the team roster). This is a real bet on a two-to-four-person shop, not a
  funded platform with runway visibility.
- No named clients, case studies, or press found anywhere in public search; everything about
  Rozeta's traction is self-reported on its own site.
- The two-posting overlap above (section 6) is itself worth being upfront about if it comes up,
  rather than pretending Santiago has not noticed.
- Gap already flagged in the prior application: no hands-on Zapier/Make/n8n experience; all of
  Santiago's automation work has been code (Python/TypeScript/MCP), which is learnable but is a
  named requirement gap.

## 9. Application-question angles

Why this company: lead with the direct overlap, not the pitch back to them. Pragma AI already is
a small, client-facing AI implementation shop doing RAG, agents, and workflow automation for
SMBs; Rozeta's "production over pilots, one engineer owns the engagement" model is what Santiago
is already doing today, just at his own company instead of theirs. Do not restate their mission
copy; state the actual overlap in work.

Why you fit: anchor on Pragma Brain (RAG with citation gating and an eval harness) and the MCP
tool-use work (Kairos, Pragma's hosted MCP service) since those map directly onto the postings'
retrieval, tool-calling, and evals requirements. Back it with the operator history (Online
Basics' internal tooling, Eugenia.ai's client deployments) rather than listing all four resume
angles.

Anything else: this is the place to be direct about the comp band if it has not come up yet, and
about the fact that he already has an application in for the Senior Agentic AI Engineer posting
from July 22. Naming it plainly (rather than letting a 2-4 person team discover two applications
on their own) is more consistent with the direct, no-fluff tone Rozeta's own postings ask for.
