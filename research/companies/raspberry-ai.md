## 1. Snapshot

Series A generative-AI design platform for fashion and apparel brands (sketch to photorealistic,
retail-ready visuals). Founded 2022 by Cheryl Liu, HQ in New York City, ~67 employees as of May
2026, fully remote-friendly hiring. Raised $28.5M total: $4.5M seed (led by Khosla Ventures) plus
a $24M Series A in January 2025 led by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), with Greycroft, Correlation
Ventures, MVP Ventures, and angels including Gokul Rajaram and Ken Pilot.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/13/raspberry-ai-raises-24m-from-a16z-to-accelerate-fashion-design/),
[Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/raspberry-ai/__Ha0mdfWQ63qT6fIYYe9_gGbdqcP8_MqpEAhoUeSi_dQ)

## 2. Core narrative

Raspberry AI is trying to compress the fashion industry's notoriously slow design-to-shelf
cycle (traditionally 12 to 18 months) using proprietary generative models fine-tuned specifically
for retail-ready output (technical drawings, CAD files, photorealistic renders), not a generic
image model wrapped in a fashion skin. That specificity is the wedge: a16z explicitly invested
because Raspberry built "an end-to-end design platform built specifically for retail product
design" with fine-tuned ControlNets rather than off-the-shelf diffusion, and the product is
already load-bearing at Under Armour and Li & Fung.
[a16z](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-raspberry-ai/). The company has since expanded
from pure design tooling into consumer-testing and marketing-content generation, i.e. moving up
the funnel from "make the sketch real" to "know what will sell before you make it," which the
founder frames as a sustainability story (less overproduction) as much as a speed story.
[Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/angelachan/2025/05/21/how-cheryl-lius-raspberry-ai-cuts-fashion-design-time-and-cost-in-half/)

- Product suite: sketch-to-render, 3D avatar conversion, a "design mixer," AI product/lifestyle
  photography and background generation, seamless print and graphic generation, on-body and
  off-body virtual try-on/visualization, an AI video studio, and a design editor.
  [raspberry.ai](https://www.raspberry.ai/)
- Business model: enterprise SaaS, tiered packages, private cloud hosting option for enterprise
  clients, brand-specific model training, emphasis on customer IP ownership and data privacy.
  [raspberry.ai](https://www.raspberry.ai/)
- Customers (named publicly): Under Armour, MCM Worldwide, Gruppo Teddy, Li & Fung, Adidas,
  Levi's, Walmart, Polo, Beyond Yoga, Columbia, Kate Spade, Terranova, Theophilio. Reported ~70

  page](https://www.raspberry.ai/press)

## 6. Engineering & AI direction

- Confirmed stack from the JD itself (this role and the identical Ashby listing): frontend in
  React / Next.js / TypeScript, backend in FastAPI / Python, RESTful APIs, GCP or AWS (serverless
  functions, containerization), BigQuery for data analysis. ML libraries (TensorFlow, PyTorch,
  Huggingface) listed as a plus, alongside hands-on LLM, prompt-engineering, and generative AI
  workflow experience.
  [Job posting](https://findwork.dev/QV5ROzn/senior-product-engineer-at-raspberry-ai)
- The role sits at the seam between product engineering and ML: "collaborate closely with machine
  learning engineers to translate prototypes into production-ready systems," i.e. this is not a
  pure ML-research seat, it is the applied/production layer that ships what ML engineers
  prototype. [Job posting](https://findwork.dev/QV5ROzn/senior-product-engineer-at-raspberry-ai)
- Core technical differentiator per the lead investor: proprietary models and fine-tuned
  ControlNets (not a generic image-gen wrapper) to guarantee retail-ready output.
  [a16z](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-raspberry-ai/)
- The company also hires a distinct Senior Machine Learning Engineer role in parallel to this
  Senior Product Engineer role, confirming an internal split between ML/model work and
  product-engineering work.
  [Ashby jobs board](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/raspberry)
- No public engineering blog was found. Their blog and press output are product/customer/industry
  focused, not technical deep dives, so the JD and Ashby board are the best available primary
  source on stack and engineering direction.
  [Blog](https://www.raspberry.ai/blog)

## 7. Why-fit angles for Santiago

Lead hook: Santiago has repeatedly built the exact seam this role sits on, full-stack product
(React/Node front end, Python/FastAPI-style backend, REST APIs) wired directly to LLM/generative
workflows, shipped to real paying users, as a founder rather than as a team member handed
requirements. At Eugenia.ai he built and shipped a React + Node full-stack SaaS platform to 20+
B2B clients and owned the whole product lifecycle including on-site client deployment. At Online
Basics he designed and ran a production LLM workflow (Anthropic and OpenAI APIs, custom prompt
engineering, multi-stage validation) that generated 100+ product listings a month. At Pragma AI he
built a RAG "company brain" and ships agents and internal automations for real clients now. That
combination, ship full-stack product AND own the generative-AI layer, maps directly onto "design,
develop, and maintain a frontend generative AI web app... and related backend services" plus
"collaborate closely with machine learning engineers to translate prototypes into
production-ready systems."

Secondary hook: founder-level ownership and 0-to-1 experience fits a 67-person Series A team that
explicitly values "Be an Owner" ("don't wait for permission") and "Bias to Solution." Santiago has
been the person responsible for outcomes (CEO/founder at two companies, 9-person team lead), not
just a task-taker, which is a good match for a small team scaling fast off a $24M raise.

Honest gaps and watch-outs:
- No fashion or apparel industry experience. The JD does not list fashion-domain knowledge as a
  requirement, but interview prep should have a point of view on why fashion (fast-cycle, visual,
  high-SKU) is a good fit for generative AI, since the company's whole narrative is
  industry-specific.
- No direct production experience with TensorFlow/PyTorch/Huggingface or fine-tuned
  ControlNets/diffusion models. Santiago's LLM experience is strong (API-level workflows, RAG,
  agents, prompt engineering) but the JD lists classic ML-framework experience as "a plus," not a
  hard requirement, and the role description frames ML-library depth as owned by the ML
  engineering team, not this role. Worth naming this directly and honestly if asked, rather than
  overclaiming ML-framework depth.
- BigQuery and GCP specifically are not called out in the resume (his cloud experience is AWS and
  Heroku); worth a one-line honest note that his cloud depth is AWS-centric and he would ramp
  quickly on GCP/BigQuery equivalents.
- Location: role is fully remote, no stated restriction to US citizens/US-only in the JD, so no
  flag against Santiago's TN-visa-eligible, Mexico-based profile, but this is worth confirming in
  the interview process since it was not explicitly addressed.

## 8. Application-question angles

- Why this company: lead with the specific technical wedge, not "I like fashion" or "AI is
  exciting." Raspberry AI bet that generic diffusion models are not good enough for retail (a16z's
  own framing), and built proprietary, fine-tuned models to make output retail-ready. Santiago's
  own pattern, building the production-grade layer around LLMs rather than treating them as a demo
  feature (the Online Basics content pipeline, the Pragma AI "company brain"), is the same bet:
  generative AI has to be engineered into something reliable enough to run a real business on, not
  just impressive in a demo.
- Why you fit: do not checklist all four research areas. Anchor on the founder-engineer pattern,
  full-stack product plus generative-AI backend, shipped and owned end to end, at Eugenia.ai and
  Online Basics, and connect it to the specific responsibility in the JD ("collaborate closely
  with machine learning engineers to translate prototypes into production-ready systems"), since
  that is precisely the founder-to-production translation work he has already done solo.
- Anything else: name the honest gap proactively and reframe it, no fashion-industry background,
  no deep TensorFlow/PyTorch production work, but real production LLM/RAG/agent experience and a
  track record of ramping fast on new domains and cloud stacks (AWS to GCP is a smaller jump than
  zero to production LLM systems, which he has already made twice). Keep it to one honest
  sentence, do not over-apologize.
