## 1. Snapshot

Truelogic is a nearshore IT staff augmentation and "Forward Deployed AI Teams" firm headquartered
in New York City, founded in 2003, with roughly 287-295 employees/contractors as of mid-2026
(third-party estimate) and reportedly 600+ tech professionals across 25+ LATAM countries in its
talent pool per its own marketing copy [tracxn.com/d/companies/truelogic; jobs.ashbyhq.com/truelogic
posting text]. No funding rounds on record; Tracxn lists it as bootstrapped/unfunded
[tracxn.com/d/companies/truelogic]. It is not itself a product company: it places engineers with US
client companies, sitting in the same competitive space as Toptal, Turing, and similar nearshore/
staff-augmentation vendors, though its "Forward Deployed Engineer" framing borrows language
popularized by Palantir-style embedded engineering roles.

## 2. Core narrative

Truelogic's pitch is speed and cost arbitrage on a well-worn nearshore staffing model, now
repackaged around AI. The company states plainly on its own site that "a forward deployed engineer
typically costs $180,000-$280,000/year fully loaded" in the US market, and that it offers "30-50%
lower cost" for the same seniority via LATAM-based, US-timezone-aligned engineers
[truelogic.io/forward-deployed-engineers]. Its homepage tagline promises shipping "production-grade
AI in weeks, not quarters" through "AI Agile Labs," forward-deployed teams embedded to attack a
single high-friction client workflow with claimed 3-5x team velocity and 30-60% reduction in manual
work [truelogic.io]. In practice this specific req is a staffing placement dressed in FDE language:
the client is not named in the posting (described only as "a global digital transformation
organization that helps leading enterprises reimagine how they create, manage, and deliver customer
experiences"), and the same underlying role is being posted simultaneously across half a dozen
LATAM cities and several English-speaking-market variants (London, Paris, Germany), which reads as
a vendor filling a recurring staffing pipeline for one or more end clients, not a single unique
engineering opening [jobs.ashbyhq.com/truelogic, full job list].

## 3. Engagement model (the critical question)

- **Business model, in Truelogic's own words**: "Nearshore Staff Augmentation is a model where
  senior LATAM engineers are embedded directly into your existing US team," working the client's
  time zone and processes; "the client selects and approves 100% of the team no matter the role"
  and "our client defines backlog and prioritization" [truelogic.io/services/nearshore-staff-
  augmentation]. This is explicit staff augmentation, i.e. Truelogic supplies and manages the
  vendor relationship while the end client directs day-to-day work, structurally different from a
  referral marketplace (which merely introduces candidate and client and steps out) and closer to a
  traditional IT outsourcing/body-shop model with an added "Trooper+" layer of non-billable project
  managers for alignment [truelogic.io/services/nearshore-staff-augmentation].
- **Employer of record**: the posting itself and the careers page do not state, in so many words,
  whether Truelogic is the legal employer or whether the engineer signs as an independent
  contractor; this is a genuine unresolved question, not something this dossier can confirm from
  public sources. The Ashby API field `employmentType: "FullTime"` describes hours/exclusivity, not
  legal classification, and does not settle the employee-vs-contractor question [api.ashbyhq.com/
  posting-api/job-board/truelogic]. Glassdoor reviews from people identifying as Truelogic
  contractors describe a distinct "contractor" track with minimal benefits ("does not offer the
  client's holidays and provides basically no other benefit than 5 days off per semester worked"),
  implying Truelogic runs at least two engagement tracks (something closer to employee, and
  something closer to 1099/contractor) and that candidates should ask explicitly which track a
  given req falls under [glassdoor.com Truelogic reviews search summary].
- **Client disclosure**: the end client is not named in the job posting, only described generically
  ("a global digital transformation organization"); this is consistent with common staffing-vendor
  practice (the vendor often cannot name the client pre-hire for contractual reasons) but is worth
  probing directly in a screening call, since "who do I actually report to and whose product am I
  shipping" materially changes the pitch this dossier can build for a cover letter
  [jobs.ashbyhq.com/truelogic posting].
- **How this differs from a rejected referral marketplace**: Santiago has previously rejected
  referral-marketplace-style sources (introduce-and-step-away models with no real employer
  relationship or accountability). Truelogic is structurally different: it operates a real,
  22-year-old company with its own delivery management layer (non-billable PMs), sub-6% reported
  attrition, and 93% client retention claims, and it takes on legal/managerial responsibility for
  the engineer rather than a one-time introduction fee [truelogic.io/services/nearshore-staff-
  augmentation]. That said, it is still fundamentally a staffing vendor selling engineer-hours to a
  US client at a markup, not a product company hiring Santiago to build its own product; that
  framing should stay explicit in any cover letter rather than being written as if Truelogic itself
  is "the company" whose mission Santiago is joining.

## 4. Pay: the 30-50%-cheaper claim versus the 70K USD LATAM floor

- Truelogic's own public pricing claim: US market rate for an FDE is "$180,000-$280,000/year fully
  loaded," and Truelogic's nearshore alternative runs "30-50% lower cost"
  [truelogic.io/forward-deployed-engineers]. Applying that discount band to the stated US range
  gives an implied Truelogic bill rate (what the client pays, not necessarily what the engineer
  takes home) of roughly $90,000 to $196,000, with the midpoint of the discount (40% off the
  $180K-$280K range) landing around $130,000-$140,000. This is Truelogic's cost-to-client framing,
  not a disclosed engineer salary; the actual take-home is very likely materially lower once
  Truelogic's own margin is taken out, and this posting discloses no number at all beyond
  "Highly Competitive USD Pay: Earn an excellent, market-leading compensation in USD"
  [jobs.ashbyhq.com/truelogic posting].
- Third-party aggregator data is inconsistent and should be treated with real skepticism: Glassdoor-
  derived estimates for "Senior Software Engineer" at Truelogic show roughly $164K-$259K, which
  looks like a US-normalized algorithmic estimate rather than a LATAM-specific figure, since it sits
  well above typical LATAM nearshore comp and above even Truelogic's own stated 30-50%-discounted
  band [glassdoor.com Truelogic Senior Software Engineer salary search summary]. Separately, an
  unverified Glassdoor/Reddit-sourced review snippet describes Truelogic employee salaries "ranging
  from $55K starting to $65K a year for employees with more than several years of experience,"
  which is a starkly lower figure and, if accurate, would sit below Santiago's 70K USD LATAM floor
  [glassdoor.com Truelogic careers/benefits search summary]. This dossier cannot resolve the gap
  between these two very different third-party numbers; treat both as unverified and get an actual
  number from a recruiter before investing further, since it directly determines whether this role
  clears the 70K USD floor at all.

- For this specific req, the disclosed "our client" language describes a global digital
