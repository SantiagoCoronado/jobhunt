## 1. Snapshot

NextLink Labs is a bootstrap-flavored, Pittsburgh-founded (2014) technology consultancy, roughly
11-50 employees on LinkedIn (other sourcing suggests it is closer to the small end of that range,
around 16-17 people across North and South America as of mid-2026, though that specific figure is
not confirmed by a primary source and should be treated as directional). Historically a
DevOps/DevSecOps and cloud-modernization shop (AWS Advanced Consulting Partner, SOC 2 Type I,
GitLab and Docker Verified Services Partner); in 2026 it repositions itself as "AI engineering for
mid-market operations," embedding senior engineers with client teams to ship production AI
workflows. No evidence of external venture funding turned up in the sources checked (company site,
press releases page, LinkedIn); it reads as a client-revenue-funded consultancy, not a VC-backed
startup. Remote-first, hiring the Forward Deployed Engineer (AI) role as a long-term independent
contractor position across LATAM (postings exist for both Mexico and Brazil).
(Sources: linkedin.com/company/nextlinklabs; nextlinklabs.com/about; nextlinklabs.com;
builtin.com cached Mexico posting; jobs.ashbyhq.com Brazil posting)

## 2. Core narrative

NextLink Labs spent a decade as a Pittsburgh DevOps/DevSecOps consultancy (GitLab named it
Emerging Partner of the Year in 2023) and is now, in 2026, converting that operational credibility
into an "AI engineering for mid-market operations" practice with the tagline "Builders, not
advisors." The pitch is specific and unusually concrete for a consultancy: fixed-price engagements,
no retainers, the client owns the code, and senior engineers work directly inside the client's
repos, stack, and security model to ship one production AI workflow (quoting, scheduling, billing)
in weeks, human-in-the-loop by design. They validate the pitch on themselves, claiming to run their
own sales pipeline on AI systems they built in-house (the "AI Quoting Accelerator," cited at a 96%
cut in quoting search time). The Forward Deployed Engineer role is how they are scaling that
practice: hiring individual contractor engineers across LATAM (Mexico, Brazil) to sit close to a
named client's ambiguous problem, prototype fast, and ship into that client's environment.
(Sources: nextlinklabs.com homepage; nextlinklabs.com/about; GitLab press release; builtin.com
cached Mexico posting)

## 6. Engineering & AI direction

- Stack signaled by the Forward Deployed Engineer (AI) job description (Mexico posting content,
  corroborated by the near-identical Brazil twin posting): Python as the primary language, plus
  TypeScript/JavaScript, Go, or Java for integration work; LLM APIs across Anthropic, OpenAI, Azure
  OpenAI, and AWS Bedrock; data engineering tooling (dbt, Airflow, Dagster, Snowflake, BigQuery,
  Databricks) for ETL/ELT pipelines; RAG patterns, embedding models, and vector stores (pgvector,
  Pinecone, Weaviate, OpenSearch); cloud platforms AWS/Azure/GCP; evaluation suites and guardrails
  for safety, cost, and latency; testing and observability for non-deterministic systems.
  (builtin.com cached Mexico posting; jobs.ashbyhq.com Brazil posting)
- Nice-to-haves explicitly listed: consulting/solutions-engineering experience, agent frameworks
  and Model Context Protocol (MCP), prompt engineering and structured-output techniques,
  infrastructure-as-code (Terraform, Pulumi, CDK), regulated-industry experience, open-source
  contributions or technical speaking. (builtin.com cached Mexico posting)
- Employment structure: long-term independent contractor, remote LATAM, with significant overlap
  with US Eastern Time expected. (builtin.com cached Mexico posting)
- Working model described on the role itself: sit close to the client's problem, discover use
  cases, prototype rapidly, harden what works, ship into the client's environment, and feed
  reusable pieces back into NextLink's internal accelerators and reference implementations so the
  broader AI practice compounds. Reports to/works alongside an "AI Architect." (fwddeploy.com job
  board summary; builtin.com cached Mexico posting)
- Broader engineering identity outside the AI practice: Kubernetes, Terraform, GitLab CI/CD,
  application containerization, cloud migrations across AWS/Azure/GCP, application security and
  DevSecOps pipeline hardening. This is the base the AI practice is grafted onto.
  (nextlinklabs.com/solutions/devops-services; linkedin.com/company/nextlinklabs)

## 7. Why-fit angles for Santiago

**Best hook to lead with:** NextLink Labs is standing up, as a formal internal practice, close to
the exact business Santiago already co-founded and runs today. Pragma AI is "a B2B AI consultancy
delivering tailor-made AI solutions to Mexican SMBs," built around a RAG "company brain" (Pragma
Brain) and client-specific automations, the same fixed-scope, embed-with-the-client, ship-a-real-
workflow model NextLink Labs describes on its own homepage ("Builders, not advisors," fixed price,
client owns the code). Santiago is not just AI-skilled; he has personally run this playbook as the
person closing and delivering the engagement, which is unusually rare for a candidate applying to
a forward-deployed role.

Supporting angles:
- Client-facing, on-site delivery track record: Eugenia.ai's product lifecycle included Santiago
  personally leading on-site client deployments (onboarding HOAs and property administrators,
  translating conflicting requirements into shipped software), which is the same muscle a Forward
  Deployed Engineer needs when "sitting close to the client's problem."
- Agentic and RAG systems already shipped: Hermes (function-calling / tool-use agent) and Plutus
  (an LLM research agent exposed through an MCP control plane, paired with deterministic
  backtesting) map directly onto the JD's core responsibilities (RAG pipelines, agentic workflows)
  and its MCP nice-to-have, which most candidates will not have hands-on.
- Full-stack production ownership from data pipeline to UI (Online Basics' cloud microservices
  processing 100K+ daily data points via 10+ third-party API integrations; Plutus's Celery/Redis
  ingestion pipelines) speaks to the "robust data infrastructure that makes AI applications work"
  half of the role, even though the specific named tools differ (see gaps below).
- Daily Claude Code / Anthropic API user, which lines up with the JD's explicit mention of the
  Anthropic API as a primary LLM provider.

**Honest gaps / watch-outs:**
- The JD's required data-engineering tooling (dbt, Airflow, Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks) is not
  evidenced anywhere in Santiago's resume; his ETL/data-pipeline experience is real but built on
  custom Python microservices and Celery/Redis rather than these specific platforms. This is a
  genuine gap to name honestly rather than imply coverage he does not have.
  Same is true for named vector stores (pgvector, Pinecone, Weaviate, OpenSearch): his resume lists
  "Embeddings & Vector Databases" generically, not a named product from that list.
- Employment is structured as a long-term independent contractor, not an employee role; worth
  confirming benefits, taxes, and continuity implications before or during the process.
- The role expects "significant overlap with US Eastern Time." Mexico City (GMT-6) versus US

  confirmed explicitly since the JD calls it out.
- Built In shows the Mexico posting removed as of 2026-07-09; worth verifying the role is still
  open before investing heavily in a tailored application.

## 8. Application-question angles

- **Why this company:** Lead with the mirror: NextLink Labs is formalizing, as a company, the exact
  model Santiago already runs at Pragma AI (embedded, fixed-scope AI delivery into a real
  business's operational workflow, not a demo). Frame the interest as wanting to do that work at
  NextLink's scale and client roster rather than as a generic "I like AI consulting" statement.
- **Why you fit:** Do not list RAG, agents, MCP, data pipelines, and client work as four separate
  bullets. Pick one concrete thread, most likely Pragma Brain (RAG company brain, shipped to real
  SMB clients) paired with Hermes/Plutus's agentic and MCP work, and narrate it as evidence he has
  already done the "discover the use case, prototype, harden, ship into the client's environment"
  loop the JD describes, not just studied it.
- **Anything else:** Proactively name the data-engineering tooling gap (dbt/Airflow/Snowflake/
  Databricks) rather than let it surface in a technical screen, then pivot to what does transfer:
  building and operating real data pipelines solo (Online Basics' 10+ API integrations, Plutus's
  ingestion workers) and a demonstrated pattern of learning a new domain's specific tools fast
  (e-commerce SEO and marketplace mechanics at Online Basics, property-management workflows at
  Eugenia.ai).
