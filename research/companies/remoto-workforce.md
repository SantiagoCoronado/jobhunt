## Read this first: agency posting, unnamed end client

**Remoto Workforce is a nearshore staffing and Employer-of-Record (EOR) agency, not a product
or engineering company.** Its business is recruiting and legally employing Mexico-based talent
on behalf of small and mid-size US and Canadian client companies, then handling that talent's
payroll, benefits, and Mexican labor compliance while the client company directs the day-to-day
work [remotoworkforce.com homepage; remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency]. The "Agentic AI
Solutions Engineer" posting almost certainly represents an unnamed US (or Canadian) client's
open role, staffed through Remoto Workforce, not a Remoto Workforce product/engineering
position. No public source (the agency's own site, its job-description landing page, or search)
names this specific client or confirms the role is currently live on Remoto Workforce's own
channels; the only place the posting was found is the Adzuna feed, whose own details page blocks
automated verification. This dossier treats the "company" as the agency and treats the actual
employer as unknown until Santiago hears back and can ask directly.

## 1. Snapshot

Remoto Workforce is a nearshore staffing and EOR agency that recruits and legally employs
Mexico-based, English-fluent professionals on behalf of small and mid-size US/Canadian
companies, across roles from accounting to construction management to (per this posting)
technical/AI roles [remotoworkforce.com; remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency]. Founded by
co-founders Rene and David Gagnon, who previously ran a printing business for 15 years and
opened a Puebla, Mexico office in 2017 before selling the printing business and starting Remoto
Workforce [remotoworkforce.com/we-are-a-recruitment-and-outsourcing-partner]. Size and HQ are
inconsistent across public sources: LinkedIn's company page lists Vancouver, BC as headquarters
and roughly 51-200 employees (about 96 listed) with 83,577 followers, while Clutch.co lists Los
Angeles, CA as headquarters, 10-49 employees, and a 2021 founding year
[linkedin.com/company/remoto-workforce; clutch.co/profile/remoto-workforce]. Both figures are
included here as unverified and conflicting rather than reconciled; neither source is
authoritative. Placements are staffing/EOR arrangements (candidates become the client's directed
worker but Remoto Workforce's legal employee), not equity or product-company employment
[remotoworkforce.com/faq].

## 2. Core narrative

Remoto Workforce's own founding story is a "we discovered the talent, then built the pipeline"
narrative: the Gagnon co-founders ran a printing company for 15 years, opened a Puebla office in
2017 to support that business, found the Mexican talent they hired there to be exceptional, sold
the printing business around COVID, and pivoted entirely to selling that same talent pipeline to
other US/Canadian small and mid-size companies as their new business
[remotoworkforce.com/we-are-a-recruitment-and-outsourcing-partner]. The pitch to client companies
is cost and speed: EOR compliance handled for them, candidates sourced and interviewed within
2-3 weeks, and up to 60-70% payroll savings versus US hiring
[remotoworkforce.com; remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency]. This is a lead-generation and
placement narrative aimed at the hiring company, not a product or mission narrative; there is no
public evidence Remoto Workforce builds or ships its own technology.

## 3. Mission & product

- Business model: nearshore staffing plus EOR. Remoto Workforce sources, screens, and presents
  candidates to a client company; once approved, Remoto Workforce becomes the candidate's legal
  employer of record (payroll, taxes, benefits, Mexican labor-law compliance) while the client
  company supervises the day-to-day work and pays Remoto Workforce in USD via ACH
  [remotoworkforce.com/faq; remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency].
- Stated mission: "Helping U.S. and Canadian companies scale smarter by connecting them with
  qualified remote talent in Mexico in a fast, compliant, and cost effective way," targeted
  explicitly at small and mid-size companies, not enterprises
  [remotoworkforce.com/we-are-a-recruitment-and-outsourcing-partner].
- Placement process (as described to client companies): (1) client defines the ideal candidate
  profile, (2) Remoto Workforce recruits and the client chooses with no fees or commitment to
  browse candidates, (3) Remoto Workforce handles onboarding, HR, and compliance after hire
  [remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency]. Typical timeline from approved go-ahead to hire is
  quoted as 2-3 weeks (elsewhere on the same site, "5-10 days" average time to hire)
  [remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency; remotoworkforce.com/faq].
- Roles/industries: the site markets placements across categories including SaaS, e-commerce,
  tech startups, logistics, fintech, and "Public Services & Education" (explicitly naming
  healthcare, alongside education and public service, as a sector they place into)
  [remotoworkforce.com/staffing-agency; remotoworkforce.com/job-descriptions]. No public page
  names AI, agentic AI, or life sciences specifically as a staffing specialty; those terms appear
  only in this one job posting, not in the agency's own marketing.
- Candidates: for hired professionals, compensation is described as competitive wages plus
  private health/life insurance and legally mandated Mexican vacation days, with the client
  billed in USD only for actual hours worked (not vacation time); hours are aligned to US
  business time zones (Central Time referenced in the posting's schedule line: 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM
  EST) [remotoworkforce.com/faq; jobhunt DB job 21102 description].
