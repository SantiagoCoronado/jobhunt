# Opusense AI

## 1. Snapshot
AI-powered inspection software that turns field notes (voice, photo, text) into formatted, firm-branded inspection reports for construction, engineering, and property-inspection teams; billed by the founders as the start of an "AI operating system for field work." Y Combinator company (X25 batch, Spring 2025), founded 2024 by Roya Cody (CEO) and Michael Bacani (CTO). Raised a $500K seed round (closed around June 2025, Scale Asia Ventures leading with Y Combinator participating). Very small team, LinkedIn shows 2-10 employees (3 visible profiles); LinkedIn lists HQ as Toronto, Canada with a secondary San Francisco presence, while a separate funding database lists a New York registration address, so exact HQ is not fully consistent across public sources. This role (job 8886, "Software Engineer") is fully remote. [opusense.com](https://www.opusense.com/), [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/opusense-ai), [startupintros.com](https://startupintros.com/orgs/opusense-ai), [LinkedIn company page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/opusense)

## 2. Core narrative
Two engineer-founders who lived the problem built the fix: Roya Cody, a former field inspector with a PhD in civil engineering, and Michael Bacani, an engineer with a background in AI systems for robotics and construction-tech startups, started by automating the single task both of them describe as "the worst part of the job," writing site inspection reports. They got into Y Combinator (X25, Spring 2025), raised a small $500K seed, shipped fast (self-serve onboarding, site-plan pin integration, auto-generated task lists), and are already deployed with "multiple multinational corporations across North America" per their own YC listing. Now, per the job description this dossier is grounded in, they are hiring their first engineer(s) beyond the two founders to help push the product from "generate one report" toward an AI layer that understands an entire organization's accumulated field knowledge, agents and RAG over thousands of past reports, not just a single-document generator. [HN Launch thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44042791), [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/opusense-ai), [findwork.dev job posting](https://findwork.dev/Q4qJjLM/software-engineer-at-opusense-ai)

## 3. Mission & product
- Product loop: field inspectors capture data via voice-to-text, photo (with GPS tagging/annotation), or typed notes on a mobile app that works offline and syncs to the cloud; AI drafts a report that "mimics your templates, phrasing, and writing style," exported instantly as Word or PDF. Tagline: "Site data in. Client report out." [opusense.com](https://www.opusense.com/)
- Platform layer beyond capture: a web dashboard with review workflows and role-based access, an AI assistant for portfolio-level questions ("Top 3 buildings by water ingress?"), site-plan markup that pins findings to exact locations, and auto-generated task lists synced to Fieldwire for closure tracking. [opusense.com](https://www.opusense.com/), [LinkedIn company page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/opusense)
- Business model: B2B SaaS sold to construction, engineering, and property-inspection firms (building science, roofing, envelope consulting, facilities, restoration are named verticals). Headline efficiency claim on the site: "80% less time on reports"; YC listing separately cites a "5x" reduction in report time. Already deployed with "multiple multinational corporations across North America" (self-reported, not independently verified). [opusense.com](https://www.opusense.com/), [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/opusense-ai)
- Stated long-term vision (per the job posting): documentation is only the first step; the goal is AI systems that understand projects, learn from organizational knowledge, surface insights across thousands of reports, and act as an "intelligent partner" for every field team. Roya Cody's current LinkedIn tagline ("AI-Native Field Reviews @ Opusense") is a public signal the product is already expanding from report generation into review/QA workflows. [findwork.dev job posting](https://findwork.dev/Q4qJjLM/software-engineer-at-opusense-ai), [Roya Cody LinkedIn, via search](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/royacody)

## 4. Culture & voice
- Extremely founder-close team: the job posting itself says a new hire will "work directly with the founders, own major technical decisions," and "help set the technical direction and standards as one of the first engineers on the team." [findwork.dev job posting](https://findwork.dev/Q4qJjLM/software-engineer-at-opusense-ai)
- Public voice (HN launch post, Product Hunt) is plainspoken and engineer-to-engineer, not hype-driven: founders describe report writing as "by far the most tedious and mind-numbing part of the job" and pitch on three concrete differentiators, built by engineers who lived the problem, offline-first reliability, and templates customized to each firm rather than generic outputs. [HN Launch thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44042791)
- On AI trust and accuracy, when challenged on Hacker News about liability if an AI-drafted report contributes to a bad outcome, the founders were direct: outputs are grounded in each firm's own historical reports (not invented from scratch), and the inspector reviews and retains final responsibility before anything goes to a client. That is a real, sourced data point on how they think about guardrails for a RAG-heavy product. [HN Launch thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44042791)
- Company philosophy on AI architecture (per LinkedIn company updates): building "purpose-built tools" that absorb the complexity of AI context management rather than "bolting AI onto" existing workflows, i.e. a stated preference against thin GPT-wrapper approaches. [LinkedIn company page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/opusense)
- No public engineering blog was found. Social presence is thin and mostly limited to the YC/Product Hunt/Hacker News launch moment plus a company LinkedIn page and an X account (@Opus_AI) with limited visible content; unverified beyond what is cited here.

## 6. Engineering & AI direction
- Stack per the job posting: Next.js, React Native (Expo), and Tailwind on the frontend; TypeScript and Python backends running on AWS Lambda; multimodal AI agents plus RAG; a relational database with vector search; automated document generation for Word, Excel, and PDF; event-driven and scheduled data pipelines. [findwork.dev job posting](https://findwork.dev/Q4qJjLM/software-engineer-at-opusense-ai)
- Team is tiny, founders plus at most a couple of others per LinkedIn (a third profile, Lucy Chen, appears alongside the two founders), so this hire genuinely shapes architecture and engineering standards rather than executing a pre-set plan. [LinkedIn company page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/opusense)
- Grounding strategy for accuracy: AI report drafts are anchored in each customer firm's own historical reports and templates rather than generated from a blank prompt, with the human inspector as the final reviewer, a deliberate choice discussed by the founders when pressed on hallucination risk. [HN Launch thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44042791)
- Stated product direction (per the job posting and Roya Cody's "AI-Native Field Reviews" LinkedIn tagline) is to move beyond single-report generation into insight and workflow AI across an organization's full report history, meaning RAG over a growing, firm-specific document corpus is expected to become more central, not less, over time. [findwork.dev job posting](https://findwork.dev/Q4qJjLM/software-engineer-at-opusense-ai), [Roya Cody LinkedIn, via search](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/royacody)

## 7. Why-fit angles for Santiago
**Best hook to lead with**: Santiago is exactly the "0 to 1 builder that has built something people used/paid for (founder/startup)" the job description explicitly asks for, and he has done it three separate times: Online Basics (scaled to $8M USD revenue on LLM-powered content workflows and automated data pipelines), Eugenia.ai (built and shipped a full-stack B2B product to 20+ buildings and 10,000+ users, then exited it), and Pragma AI, where he is currently building and shipping a RAG "company brain" product (Pragma Brain) plus conversational AI agents for real clients today. That last one in particular is a close structural match to what Opusense is doing: a small team turning a company's own unstructured operational data into a queryable, trustworthy AI product for paying customers.

Supporting angles:
- Direct RAG and AI-agent build experience: Pragma Brain (RAG over a company's internal data to answer questions and auto-generate reports), the Kairos MCP server and in-app Claude agent, and the Plutus autonomous research agent with an MCP control plane all demonstrate the "multimodal AI agents + RAG" and "relational databases with vector search" line items in the JD, not just familiarity with the concepts.
- Document automation instinct: Online Basics ran an LLM pipeline with a multi-stage validation step generating 100+ structured product listings per month; Eugenia.ai required turning "messy, real-world" and often conflicting client requirements into working software. Both are the same muscle the JD asks for when it says "turn messy, real-world field data into clear, useful reports and insights."
- End-to-end ownership with direct user contact: Eugenia.ai's on-site HOA and property-administrator deployments and Online Basics's client-facing operations match the JD's "own features end-to-end: speak directly with users, ship prototypes fast, and refine them into what customers rely on."
- Fully remote fits Santiago's Mexico City base with no relocation required, and he is bilingual (native Spanish, fluent English) and TN-visa eligible if the role or its structure ever touches US employment.

- No AEC (architecture, engineering, construction) domain experience. The JD lists this as a bonus, not a requirement, but it is a real gap worth naming rather than glossing over.
- No React Native/Expo (mobile) experience is visible in the resume; the JD's stack includes mobile, and the existing internal match verdict already flags this as lighter for him.
- The company is extremely early: a single seed round of $500K, a team of roughly 2 to 10 people, and no Series A found publicly as of this research date. Runway, stability, and equity terms are unverifiable from public sources and would need to be probed directly in the process; this dossier cannot confirm or deny financial health beyond the amount raised.

Santiago's closest public evidence is his own resume-rendering pipeline (python-docx based), which is directionally relevant but not the same as production document-templating experience at scale for paying customers; worth being precise about this distinction rather than overstating it.
