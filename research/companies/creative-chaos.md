## 1. Snapshot

Privately held IT services and consulting firm, founded 2000, corporate presence in Boston, MA
and San Francisco, CA, delivery staff concentrated in Pakistan (Karachi and Lahore). LinkedIn
lists company size as 201 to 500 employees; the company's own "about" copy elsewhere claims
"300+ full-time associates" and "~150 engineers, designers, and operators." No disclosed
external funding found; founder-led and appears self-funded (privately held, no VC/press
coverage of a raise). In June 2026 it launched "AI Orchestrator," a new billable service line
selling embedded process-automation consultants to mid-market ($5 to $50 million revenue)
clients, alongside existing "Forward Deployed Engineer" and "Squads" offerings.

## 2. Core narrative

Creative Chaos is a 25-year-old Pakistan-delivered custom software shop (web, mobile, MVPs for
Fortune 500s and startups) that has repackaged itself around AI language in 2026: it now sells
"AI Orchestrators" who embed inside a client's operations, map the highest-cost manual workflows
in 30 days, and ship a production automation by day 60, billed as a share of the first-year value
identified. The pitch is explicitly anti-consulting ("we are not advisors, we are accountable for
execution") and anti-outsourcing in its marketing, even though the underlying company is
structurally a global delivery/staff-augmentation firm whose job board is nearly entirely
Pakistan-based. [[creativechaos.co/ai-orchestrator]](https://creativechaos.co/ai-orchestrator/)
[[linkedin.com/company/creative-chaos]](https://www.linkedin.com/company/creative-chaos)

## 8. What a Principal AI Process Orchestrator actually does day to day

Per the job posting itself (not summarized secondhand):

- Owns 3 to 4 concurrent client engagements as the face of each one, sitting with the client's
  COO or CEO from week one.
- First 30 days of each engagement: maps how work flows through the client's org, produces an
  "opportunity map" of the 5 to 10 workflows where AI could materially change unit economics.
- Designs the future state for 2 to 3 selected workflows: what humans do, what agents do,
  handoffs, evaluations, success metrics.
- Scopes the build, directs "Associate Orchestrators" on the engagement, reviews their output,
  ships the first automation to production within 60 days of engagement start.
- Identifies upsell into a Build pod, a Rescue, or ongoing Support, and pulls in the right
  internal resource.
- Reports outcomes as hard numbers: hours saved, error rates reduced, throughput gained.

This is explicitly a senior individual-contributor, billable, client-facing consulting role, not
a management role and not a hands-on engineering seat: "We are hiring the sharpest business
analyst you know, the one who also spends their weekends building AI agents."

## 10. Comp assessment for the LATAM req

The posting itself states: "Compensation open and based on capability and experience," no band,
no floor. [[apply.workable.com/creativechaos/j/DCBA553E8C]](https://apply.workable.com/creativechaos/j/DCBA553E8C/)

Risk factors worth naming plainly:

- The company's core business model depends on the spread between what it bills clients (US and
  Western mid-market companies) and what it pays delivery staff, most of whom sit in Pakistan, a
  low-cost geography. Glassdoor reviews from that geography mention pay below market rate.
- The LATAM req for this exact title is brand new (posted 2026-08-15, a month after the
  Pakistan-only version) and covers six countries in one requisition, consistent with the company
  testing where it can source this profile cheaply rather than having priced a Mexico-specific
  band.
- No public comp data exists yet for this role in any LATAM country.

None of this proves the pay will land below the $70,000 USD annual floor, and the role itself
(client-facing, "Principal," business-outcome-owning) is priced above typical delivery-staff
roles in the same company. But given the offshore-arbitrage business model and the total absence
of a posted band, comp clearing the floor is genuinely uncertain, not a safe assumption. This
should be raised directly and early in the process, before investing in the 48-hour take-home
exercise.

## 11. Why-fit angles for Santiago

**Best hook to lead with:** Santiago already ran the loop this role describes, as the owner of
the business, not as a hired consultant. At Online Basics he found where manual work was costing
money (content creation, pricing, reporting), redesigned the workflow around an LLM pipeline and
automated data sync, and shipped it: content-creation time cut 70%, manual reporting cut from
hours to minutes, data-entry errors cut 90%. That is the exact "map the business, redesign the
workflow, ship the automation, report hours saved and error rates" loop the job description asks
for, and he has the numbers to back it because he was the P&L owner, not a vendor.

**Other real matches:**
- "Deeply AI-native, portfolio over resume" is close to a checklist match: Pragma Brain (a RAG
  system with citation gating and an eval harness), Kairos (an MCP server exposing 52 tools to
  Claude Code), and Plutus (an MCP control plane for an LLM research agent) are shipped, public,
  and demonstrate exactly the "built real agents, not just uses ChatGPT" bar the posting sets.
- Client-facing ownership: onboarded HOAs and property administrators at Eugenia.ai, ran client
  diagnostics and delivery at Pragma AI, matches the "sit across from the COO in week one" and
  "own the weekly update, the monthly business review" expectations.
- Family B fit per his own profile (AI deployment/enablement/strategy) is a strong match for this
  specific title; his profile explicitly flags this family as in-scope and not a seniority
  mismatch.

**Honest concerns and watch-outs:**
- Comp is unposted and the business model raises real risk it lands below the $70,000 floor (see
  section 10). This is the top thing to resolve early, not late.
- The role is IC billable-consulting work carrying 3-4 concurrent clients with a hard 60-day
  production SLA per engagement, a heavier and more externally-paced workload than his strongest
  two lanes (frontend, internal tooling), and closer to pure client-facing delivery than to
  hands-on building.
- The "we chose Mexico" story does not hold up: this exact title is being sourced simultaneously
  across six LATAM countries and separately in Pakistan, so a cover letter should not lean on any
  sense that Creative Chaos specifically targeted the Mexico market. Lead with the work, not the
  company's location strategy.
- Company self-reported size and project-count figures are inconsistent across its own channels
  (see section 5), worth a light mental discount on any other unverified claim from the company
  during interviews.

## 12. Application-question angles

**Why this company:** Lead with the mechanics of the role, not the company pitch. He has already
run the "find the manual work, redesign it, ship the automation, report the numbers" loop as a
business owner (Online Basics), and the AI Orchestrator role is that same loop done for someone
else's business, with a real 60-day production deadline attached.

**Why you fit:** Anchor on measured outcomes he actually owned (70% cut in content-creation time,
90% cut in data-entry errors, manual reporting cut from hours to minutes at Online Basics) paired
with a portfolio of shipped agent systems (a RAG brain with an eval harness, an MCP server
exposing dozens of tools to Claude Code) that answers the "portfolio over resume, show us a repo"
bar directly.

**Anything else:** Ask about compensation structure early since none is posted and flag that a
floor needs to be cleared for a LATAM-based hire; also worth asking directly how the six-country
LATAM req is being staffed (one hire across six countries, or several), since that shapes what
"choosing this role" actually means.
