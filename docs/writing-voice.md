# Writing voice for application text

Applies to every human-facing thing we generate: form answers, cover letters, follow-up
emails, LinkedIn messages. Written after Santiago rejected a batch of answers as "a bad
LinkedIn post written by ChatGPT" (2026-07-21). He was right.

Target voice: a sharp engineer typing an answer into a form. Plain, specific, slightly
casual, done when the facts are done. Not a keynote, not a personal brand.

## Rules

1. **Say the fact first.** No windup, no thesis statement. Never open by restating the
   company's pitch back to them ("Your bet is that...", "That is the problem X sells").
   They know what their company does.
2. **No reversal constructions.** "Not X, it is Y" / "I don't just X, I Y" is the single
   loudest AI tell. Zero per document is the target.
3. **No kicker endings.** No closing fragment, no aphorism, no "what I took from it is
   that...". When the last fact is stated, stop.
4. **No invented precision.** Never make up percentages, ratios, or splits ("the sync was
   30% of the work"). Numbers only if they were actually measured and are in the master
   resume or verified repos.
5. **No rhetorical rhythm.** Rule-of-three lists and anaphora built for cadence get cut.
   A list of real facts is fine; a list shaped for applause is not.
6. **No self-narrated virtue.** Delete "I would rather say that plainly", "to be honest",
   "I know from experience", "work I would find genuinely satisfying". Honesty is shown by
   the content, not announced.
7. **Intensifier ban:** genuinely, truly, deeply, incredibly, "real"/"really" as
   emphasis. If the sentence dies without the intensifier, the sentence was empty.
8. **Contractions are fine. Drama fragments are not.** "I didn't" reads human. "Nobody
   asked me to." as its own paragraph beat reads like a script.
9. **When detail is thin, get shorter, not grander.** Abstraction is what fills the gap
   between word count and knowledge. Don't fill the gap.
10. **One sentence of why-them is plenty.** Spend the rest on concrete things Santiago
    did. No flattery.
11. **Read-aloud test.** If a sentence would work as a LinkedIn post opener or a keynote
    line, rewrite it as if answering a colleague's question in chat.
12. **Length default is short.** A form answer over ~150 words needs a reason (e.g. the
    question explicitly has four parts).
13. Zero em-dashes (existing house rule, still canonical).
14. **Name projects only when the name carries information for the reader.** "Kairos
    exposes 52 tools to Claude Code; Plutus has an MCP control plane" in a form answer
    reads like a portfolio slide written by AI (Santiago flagged exactly this, 2026-08-24).
    Say what was built ("MCP servers that let agents operate the products I've built");
    the names live in the resume and repos, or come up when a question is about one
    specific project.

## Quick self-check before delivering

Scan the draft for: "not X, it's Y" / a closing one-liner / a made-up percentage /
"genuinely" / three parallel clauses in a row. Any hit means rewrite, not patch.
